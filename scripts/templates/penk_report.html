hm2p · retrosplenial cortex · cell-type comparison

Penk+ and Penk⁻CamKII+ neurons in the Rosenberg maze

Two-photon GCaMP7f imaging in freely moving mice, 1 min light / 1 min dark, plus ex vivo whole-cell recordings. Analyses run 2026-09-25 to 2026-10-02. Exploratory results, not peer reviewed. Build __BUILD__.

Penk+ (Cre-ON, 11 animals) Penk⁻CamKII+ (Cre-OFF, 4 animals)

Summary

Penk+ cells fire long, rare events while the mouse is running, and report whether it is running rather than how fast. Penk⁻CamKII+ cells follow the room lights more closely.

  • CellsThe mouse runs faster than chance during the events of 52 % of Penk+ cells vs 26 % of Penk⁻CamKII+ cells. 58 % vs 35 % of cells step up when the mouse runs; only 7 % vs 4 % track graded speed, which is chance.
  • CellsPenk+ events are longer (3.1 vs 2.6 s) and rarer (interval 30 vs 25 s) with the same isolated-event decay (0.31 vs 0.31 s). Penk+ cells are about twice as bright, and their smaller amplitude and lower inferred rate cannot be separated from that.
  • CellsPenk⁻CamKII+ cells are more light-modulated (light modulation, lights-on response, GLM light share; Cliff's δ −0.12 to −0.16) and lose more of their AHV modulation in the dark (dF/F units, so partly brightness-dependent).
  • AnimalsWith each animal as one data point (11 vs 4) most of these lean the same way without reaching significance alone; rank composites reach p = 0.037 (running, Penk+ higher) and p = 0.009 (light, Penk⁻CamKII+ higher), defined post hoc.
  • MazeIn the maze, Penk+ activity marks travelling bouts, not where the mouse goes: no preference for corridors vs junctions, runs into vs out of dead ends, or new vs familiar territory, and only weak, shared route specificity. A Penk+ event (3.1 s) usually spans several runs (median 1.3 s).

The report has two parts. Part 1 pools every cell and compares the two cell types cell by cell, ignoring which animal a cell came from. Part 2 treats each animal as one data point. Cells from the same animal share expression level, imaging conditions and behaviour, so Part 1 p values are much smaller than the evidence justifies; Part 2 is the inferential test, and it is weak because there are only 4 Penk⁻CamKII+ animals.

Part 1

Cell by cell

309 Penk+ cells from 11 animals and 141 Penk⁻CamKII+ cells from 4 animals, pooled. Running in the maze

In this maze a run is a short corridor traversal between dead ends and junctions. Penk+ cells care that the mouse is travelling, not where it is going.

Maze graph (23 cells)

The maze is a tree: 23 cells, 9 dead ends, 7 T-junctions and 7 corridor cells, with no loops. Every route between two dead ends passes through junctions, and the mouse's running is mostly back-and-forth runs along these short corridors, slowing at junctions and turning around in dead ends.

What the data say now. Penk+ event onsets are biased to corridors, but at matched running speed the corridor-vs-junction index is near zero (median −0.015 in both cell types) and the few significant cells split both ways (11 % vs 17 %). Within corridors, faster movement still raises Penk+ activity (median index 0.071; 19 % of cells). So location in the maze adds nothing beyond running.

What the run-state code could mean in the maze. A cell that switches on for the duration of a run and does not care about speed marks "travelling along a corridor" as a unit. In a tree maze each such run is one step of a route choice. If that is what Penk+ cells carry, their activity should depend on what the run is in the maze (where it goes, whether it is new) and not only on the fact of running. If it does not, they carry a generic locomotor state.

What the maze analysis found

Penk+ activity tracks running wherever and however it happens in the maze: it does not care whether the run goes into or out of a dead end, into new or familiar territory, and it is only weakly route-specific, no more than Penk⁻CamKII+ cells.

  • Coupling to running is the only strong signal. Penk+ activity is correlated with the run indicator in 51 % of cells (median z 1.9; animal-level p = 0.001), more than Penk⁻CamKII+ (37 %, z 1.2; between cell types p < 0.001 on cells, 0.10 on animals).
  • Where the run goes does not matter. Runs into vs out of dead ends: median z ≈ 0 in both, 13–16 % of cells individually significant with mixed signs. New vs visited territory and repeated traversals of the same corridor: no effect in Penk+.
  • Route specificity is weak and shared. Per-route activity is slightly more consistent than shuffled routes in both cell types (median z 0.34 vs 0.39), but only 8–12 % of cells pass individually, close to the 5 % expected by chance. This is a small population-wide effect, not a Penk+ route code.
  • No lead or lag. Activity rises with running, not before it; there is no preparatory signal at this time resolution (~0.1 s frames, CASCADE).
  • Slightly more in the light. Penk+ cells are a little more active on light runs than on dark runs at matched speed (z 0.33 on cells; not significant across animals), so optic flow may add a small amount on top of self-motion.
  • Events span runs. Most runs last 1.1–1.7 s (median 1.3 s, 4003 runs in 23 sessions); a median Penk+ event lasts 3.1 s. A Penk+ event therefore typically covers more than one run, plus the pauses at junctions in between.

What this means in the maze. The Penk+ signal is not tied to a place, a route, a goal (dead end) or novelty. It marks that the mouse is in a bout of travelling through the maze, at a timescale of several seconds that spans individual corridor runs. The simplest reading is a locomotor state signal (the mouse is moving through the maze now) rather than a navigational variable. Two things it could still feed: integrating self-motion over a bout (path integration needs exactly a "currently moving" gate), or marking travel epochs for later memory. Neither is tested here.

Discrete type or continuum?

Penk+ cells do not form a separate cluster. Their distributions overlap Penk⁻CamKII+ at the low end and stretch further at the high end of running coupling and event length, which fits a continuum better than a discrete type.

So far "Penk+" has been treated as a category. Layer 2/3 excitatory neurons in transcriptomic atlases mostly vary along gradients rather than falling into clean clusters, and a Cre-ON / Cre-OFF split cuts any such gradient at a threshold. Silverman's test of one mode against more (Silverman 1981); the uncalibrated version used here is conservative (Hall and York 2001), and with 20 tests about one p < 0.05 is expected by chance. Timescales are tested on a log scale.

  • No hidden sub-population. No measure is clearly bimodal in either label. If Penk+ were a distinct type mixed into a population, or Penk⁻ a mixture of distinct types, some running or timing measure would be expected to split.
  • The difference opens in the upper half. The lowest Penk+ cells look like Penk⁻ cells; the gap widens toward the top deciles for running coupling, step index, event duration and interval (event duration: about 9 % longer at the 10th percentile, about 50 % at the 90th). Penk+ covers a wider range that extends further, rather than sitting at a separate location.
  • Penk⁻ cells sit closer together than Penk+ cells. Among neighbours drawn from other animals, Penk⁻ cells have more Penk⁻ neighbours than label permutation predicts (p ≈ 0.06–0.07); Penk+ cells show no clustering. This matches the earlier finding that Penk+ cells are more dispersed.
  • The axes are not opposite ends of one variable. Running and light scores are weakly positively correlated across cells, so "running cells" are not simply "non-visual cells".
  • A graded hint, with a caveat. Within Penk+, brighter cells score higher on the running axis (ρ = 0.16, p = 0.005), but not within Penk⁻. Brightness could track Cre or reporter level, but it also raises signal-to-noise and so the detectability of coupling.

The working description is a smaller, narrower-spiking layer 2/3 pyramid that fires a burst and then adapts.

Part 2

Animal level

The two cell types are never in the same animal, so a difference between them is also a difference between animals. Here each animal is reduced to one value (the median or mean of its cells) and the groups are compared with an exact animal-level Mann-Whitney U, an animal-level permutation test and a leave-one-animal-out (LOAO) direction check. Effect size is CLES = P(Penk+ animal > Penk⁻CamKII+ animal); 0.5 means no difference. With 11 vs 4 animals an exact two-sided test cannot go below p = 0.0015, and 80 % power needs about d = 1.5.

Chart statistics are recomputed from animal medians. Tables report the programme's own tests, which summarise each animal by its cell mean, so the two can differ.

Composite scores

Averaging ranks across measures, both differences reach p < 0.05 at the animal level, with every measure leaning the same way.

Composite rank per animal (higher = more coupled)

For each measure the animal median is oriented so that higher means more coupled, ranked across the 15 animals, and averaged. The two composites are uncorrelated across animals (ρ = −0.08), so they describe separate properties, and both directions hold within each equipment configuration. With a Bonferroni correction for two composites, permutation p is 0.074 (running) and 0.018 (light). The definitions are now fixed for testing in new animals.

One heterogeneity result survives removal of animal means: Penk+ cells are more dispersed in feature space within animals (dispersion ratio 0.80, p = 0.003), the opposite of the expectation that a single transcriptomic type would be the more compact population.

Interpretation

  • Penk+ as a sparse, sustained run-state population. Seconds-long events that coincide with running, follow the run/still state and persist through a run fit cells that report "the animal is running" rather than a continuous variable. In the maze the signal does not depend on where the run goes, and a 3 s event usually spans several 1–2 s corridor runs, so it marks a bout of travelling rather than a single corridor.
  • Penk⁻CamKII+ as more visually coupled. This fits a mixture of excitatory types that includes cells receiving visual or landmark input, as reported for dysgranular RSP (Jacob et al. 2017). Because the composites were defined post hoc and the groups were labelled with different constructs, this is a lead to confirm.
  • Burst firing and enkephalin (speculative). Burst-then-adapt firing produces clustered spikes at the start of a drive, the mode generally needed for neuropeptide release, so Penk+ cells could release enkephalin during runs. Nothing here measures peptide release.

Limitations

  • 11 vs 4 animals; most between-group results lean consistently without being significant alone.
  • Different constructs (Cre-ON vs Cre-OFF) and a twofold brightness difference; amplitude, inferred rate and the light composite are not controlled for construct.
  • Ex vivo cell types come from largely separate mice (effectively 5 vs 2).
  • CASCADE was trained on other cell types; inferred rates partly track brightness.
  • Composites are exploratory, and some measures share data (light-on/off from the same transitions; speed and movement during the same events).
  • Running and visual flow are confounded in a freely moving animal.
  • The overhead image is assumed unmirrored; only left/right labels depend on this, no group comparison does.
  • The mixed-model level of reporting was not computed (statsmodels not installed). Imaging depth and anterior–posterior position are not recorded per session.

Next steps

  1. Image more Penk⁻CamKII+ animals and apply the two fixed composites as the confirmatory test.
  2. Record Penk+ and Penk⁻ cells in the same slices to separate cell type from mouse.
  3. Head-fixed treadmill imaging with controlled visual flow, to separate running from optic flow and light.
  4. Construct-matched labelling (same promoter and indicator, or dual colour in one animal) to remove the brightness confound.
