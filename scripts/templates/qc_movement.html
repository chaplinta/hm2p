Two-minute excerpt

Head direction, speed and angular head velocity

Two minutes around the light switch nearest the middle of the session, at 15 Hz. Top: fused head direction (the analysis variable) with the ear-based and nose-neck estimates; wrap-around jumps between 0° and 360° are expected. Middle: body and head speed. Bottom: angular head velocity. Brief spikes in AHV that coincide with a jump between estimators point to tracking errors rather than real head turns.

Head direction

Agreement with the ear-based estimate

Circular difference of each estimator from the head direction perpendicular to the ear axis. A narrow peak at 0° means the keypoints agree on the head's axis; a constant offset reflects geometry (e.g. head-midpoint not on the ear midline); mass near ±180° means a front/back flip.

Head-direction confidence

Weight of the estimators that contributed to the fused head direction in each frame (0–1), lights on vs off.

Head-direction occupancy

Fraction of frames in each 10° sector. A strong bias toward a few directions is expected from the maze corridors; a single dominant direction may indicate a stuck estimate.

Position

Occupancy (body position)

Log-scaled frame counts in a 40 × 40 grid over the central 99.8 % of positions. The maze outline should be recognisable; isolated bright cells away from corridors suggest position errors.

Filtering and position checks

"Filtered vs raw" is the distance between the confidence-filtered, interpolated, median-smoothed body position and the unfiltered tracker position, in maze cells. Large values mean filtering replaced many frames.

How to read this

  • Frame timing: gaps are steps longer than twice the median frame interval. Any gap is a warning; non-monotonic timestamps are a problem (the schema forbids them).
  • Head direction missing: warning above 2 %, problem above 10 % of frames.
  • Nose-neck vs ears, median |Δ|: warning above 20°, problem above 40°; |Δ| > 45°: warning above 10 %, problem above 25 %.
  • Implausible speed (> 100 cm/s) and AHV (|AHV| > 1500 °/s): warning above 0.1 %, problem above 1 % of frames. These are almost always tracking jumps that survived filtering.
  • Active fraction: frames with body speed above the session's active threshold. Shown light and dark; a large difference is behaviour, not a processing error, but very low values in both suggest a scale or speed error.
  • Champion: whether the session's kinematics were computed from the current DLC champion model. Sessions from an older model are stale and should be re-run before analysis.
