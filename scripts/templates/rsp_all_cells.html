What RSP neurons respond to in the maze

Two-photon imaging of layer 2/3 retrosplenial neurons in freely moving mice exploring the Rosenberg maze, room lights alternating 1 min on / 1 min off. All soma ROIs pooled, not split by cell type. Exploratory results. Build __BUILD__.

Summary

RSP layer 2/3 neurons in the maze are dominated by locomotion: about half of all cells track whether the mouse is running. Next come slow drift over the session, light, and entry into junctions and dead ends. Head direction, turns, novelty and junction choices are weak or absent.

  • CommonRunning vs still, coupling to running and speed during events: 44–50 % of cells (animal-level p < 0.001). These responses cluster in the same cells.
  • PresentSlow drift over the session (36 %), lights on during events (24 %), junction and dead-end entry (18 % each, half up, half down), into vs out of dead ends (15 %).
  • PopulationThe population, but no simple single-cell average, tells whether the mouse is heading into or out of a dead end, and this survives removing head direction (balanced accuracy 0.61–0.64 vs 0.49 chance). It does not predict which exit the mouse takes at a junction.
  • TranslationCells respond to running straight (45 % up) far more than to turning in place (10 %); graded turning at matched speed is at chance. Their syllable information is mostly about syllable speed.
  • Absent or weakLeft and right turn responses, graded speed within runs, novelty, head-body angle and route specificity are at chance across animals. Head-direction cells are 10 % overall but concentrated in a few animals.

Data

soma ROIs from 23 sessions in 15 mice (11 Penk-Cre, 4 Cre-OFF CamKII; pooled here). Every cell was tested against its own shuffle null for each variable (a circular shift of activity relative to behaviour, or the analysis's own permutation), so about 5 % of cells pass any test by chance. The animal-level test asks whether the fraction of responsive cells per mouse exceeds 5 % (Wilcoxon signed-rank across mice), which does not treat cells as independent.

What goes together

Locomotion responses share cells: a cell that steps up with running is also more likely to be faster during its events and to be active with head turning.

Running vs turning

RSP cells encode translation, not rotation: activity rises when the mouse runs straight, barely when it turns in place, and turning during a run slightly lowers it.

In this maze the mouse turns while moving more than it runs straight, and turning in place is short (median 60 s per session, range 6–168 s). Frames are classed as still (speed < 2 cm/s, |AHV| < 30 deg/s), running straight (≥ 4 cm/s, < 30 deg/s), turning in place (< 2 cm/s, ≥ 90 deg/s) and running while turning (≥ 4 cm/s, ≥ 90 deg/s), with bouts of at least 0.3 s. Within running, speed and turning are uncorrelated (ρ ≈ 0), so the contrasts separate them.

Population decoding

Pooling every cell in a session, the population carries whether the mouse is heading into or out of a dead end, beyond head direction, speed and turning. It does not carry the upcoming junction choice.

Comparing Penk+ with Penk⁻CamKII+ on every variable, at matched brightness: Penk+ has more running cells, the same rate of head-direction and maze-event cells, and fewer light and stopping cells. It is not simply more responsive overall.

Limitations

  • Variables come from different analyses on different signals (CASCADE spikes, dF/F events, dF/F), each with its own null, so the percentages are comparable only roughly.
  • Junction and dead-end entry responses are not speed-controlled; part of them may reflect slowing on arrival.
  • Head direction significance comes from an earlier analysis on the whole session (dF/F); about half the HD cells come from a few animals.
  • The same neurons can appear in several sessions of one mouse; the animal-level tests are the safer guide.

Methods

Sources and statistics

Built by scripts/make_rsp_all_cells.py and scripts/build_rsp_all_cells_html.py from the programme runners (runshape, mazerun, etb, evt, ego, tctx, popdec) and the head-direction controls in results/headline_controls. Co-occurrence: Fisher exact test and Haldane-corrected log odds ratio within cells tested for both variables. Number of variables per cell: observed distribution against a null permuting each variable's labels across cells within each session (1000 permutations). Population decoders: L2 logistic regression, time-block cross-validation, circular-shift null; see docs/population-maze-decoding.md.
