RSP activity during maze exploration

Two-photon imaging of layer 2/3 retrosplenial neurons in mice freely exploring the Rosenberg maze (a tree with 9 dead ends and 7 T-junctions), room lights alternating 1 min on / 1 min off. All imaged soma ROIs pooled ( cells, sessions, mice). Exploratory analyses run 2026-10-03 to 2026-10-07. Build __BUILD__.

Data status

These results predate two data fixes now in progress (2026-10-06 QC). (1) The stored head direction pointed roughly backwards in about 15 % of frames (and 45–135° off in another 17 %) because of an ear-based estimator bug; numbers conditioned on head direction will change after Stages 3 → 5 → 6 are re-run. The core place-by-direction result does not depend on it: travel direction computed from the trajectory gives the same gain in all 15 mice, and the reference-frame and map analyses use travel direction. (2) The original 9 Penk sessions used fixed-coefficient neuropil subtraction while the 17 reprocessed sessions, which include every Penk⁻ session, used FISSA; any Penk vs Penk⁻ comparison is confounded by processing until one method is applied to all 26 sessions.

Summary

    Approach

    The question was what RSP population activity carries about how mice explore the maze, beyond the locomotion and head-direction signals already characterised, and how that changes when the lights go out. Candidate signals were memory (how long since a place was visited), planning (whether the next trip goes somewhere new), trip structure (where a trip started and where it ends, how far is left) and maze-relative direction.

    Every test was run on all cells together, each session decoded or tested on its own, with a circular time-shift null (activity shifted relative to behaviour by at least 30 s). Each neural result was compared with a decoder using behaviour alone (head direction, speed, turning, and, where relevant, time in session, dwell time and posture syllables) and with the neural population after removing head direction from each cell. Inference is across sessions and across mice (Wilcoxon signed-rank on per-animal medians).

    Follow-up tests of the place-by-heading code

Cells split into two roughly equal groups, one keeping a constant compass preference and one keeping an "away from vs toward the maze centre" preference. Maps are similar in light and dark. Decoding is too weak at single-frame resolution to test drift, anticipation or links to behaviour.

    • Why the dynamics tests are weak. Frame-by-frame decoding from about 20 cells is barely above chance (probability of the true maze cell 0.073 vs 0.062), whereas the earlier above-chance position and direction decoding averaged over whole visits or runs. Drift, anticipation and confidence need frame-level precision, so these are failures to detect, not evidence of absence.
    • Head direction vs maze coordinates. In 17 of 23 sessions the head-direction angle appears mirrored relative to the maze coordinates, and some sessions are offset by about ±90°. Analyses here use head direction only as a within-session category, so they are unaffected, but any analysis that maps head-direction angles onto maze geometry needs this alignment checked first.

    Prior work

    Conjunctive responses to place and heading in retrosplenial cortex have been described before in rats recorded with tetrodes: cells combining head direction, place and movement (Cho and Sharp 2001), and cells coding position along a route together with position in the room (Alexander and Nitz 2015). Head-direction responses anchored to the local environment rather than to a global compass were reported in dysgranular retrosplenial cortex (Jacob et al. 2017), which is the closest relative of the maze-centre group here.

    What these data add, if confirmed after the data fixes: identified layer 2/3 cells imaged in freely moving mice exploring a tree maze; a conjunction of equal strength in light and in total darkness, so it does not depend on vision; and, within one population, a compass-anchored group and a maze-structure-anchored group of similar size, the latter surviving speed and acceleration controls. These references were cited from memory and the recent literature has not been searched systematically.

    • Cho J, Sharp PE. 2001. "Head direction, place, and movement correlates for cells in the rat retrosplenial cortex." Behavioral Neuroscience 115:3–25. doi:10.1037/0735-7044.115.1.3
    • Alexander AS, Nitz DA. 2015. "Retrosplenial cortex maps the conjunction of internal and external spaces." Nature Neuroscience 18:1143–1151. doi:10.1038/nn.4058
    • Jacob PY, Casali G, Spieser L, Page H, Overington D, Jeffery K. 2017. "An independent, landmark-dominated head-direction signal in dysgranular retrosplenial cortex." Nature Neuroscience 20:173–175. doi:10.1038/nn.4465

      What it means

      • Maze-relative direction is the clearest non-locomotor signal. At matched speed and duration, and with head direction removed, the population still separates runs toward dead ends from runs away from them. Because head direction in the lab frame is controlled, this is direction relative to the maze's structure (toward the end of a branch vs back toward the junctions), not compass heading.
      • No evidence for planning or episodic trip memory at this resolution. Pre-departure activity, trip origin, trip destination, distance to go and time since a dead end was last visited were either not decodable or explained by posture, travel direction or immediate re-entries.
      • The behaviour is not memory-guided at the trip level. Mice reached dead ends they had not recently visited at the rate expected of a random walk, in light and in dark, and far below a walker that never reverses. A neural planning signal would have little behavioural choice to predict.

      Limitations

      • About 20 cells per session, 9.6 Hz imaging and calcium-derived activity: brief or sparse signals (e.g. sequences within a second) are below what these data can resolve.
      • The maze is small (23 cells) and trips are short; strata needed for trip-origin decoding had too few samples in most sessions.
      • Light and dark analyses each use half the data, so a null in one condition is weaker evidence than a null in pooled data.
      • Many analyses were tried; results are reported with their controls, but the surviving result should be confirmed in new data.

      Code, statistics and references

      Population decoders: hm2p.analysis.population_maze (runner popdec); memory and planning decoders: hm2p.analysis.maze_memory (runner mazemem); dead-end recency: hm2p.analysis.dead_end_recency and scripts/run_dead_end_recency.py; this page: scripts/make_rsp_maze_report.py and scripts/build_rsp_maze_report_html.py. Decoders are L2-regularised logistic regressions with balanced class weights, cross-validated over five contiguous time blocks with 5 s gaps, scored by balanced accuracy, against 200 circular shifts.
