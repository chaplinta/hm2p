RSP activity during maze exploration

Two-photon imaging of layer 2/3 retrosplenial neurons in mice freely exploring the Rosenberg maze (a tree with 9 dead ends and 7 T-junctions), room lights alternating 1 min on / 1 min off. All imaged soma ROIs pooled ( cells, sessions, mice). Exploratory analyses run 2026-10-03 to 2026-10-05. Build __BUILD__.

Summary

    Approach

    The question was what RSP population activity carries about how mice explore the maze, beyond the locomotion and head-direction signals already characterised, and how that changes when the lights go out. Candidate signals were memory (how long since a place was visited), planning (whether the next trip goes somewhere new), trip structure (where a trip started and where it ends, how far is left) and maze-relative direction.

    Every test was run on all cells together, each session decoded or tested on its own, with a circular time-shift null (activity shifted relative to behaviour by at least 30 s). Each neural result was compared with a decoder using behaviour alone (head direction, speed, turning, and, where relevant, time in session, dwell time and posture syllables) and with the neural population after removing head direction from each cell. Inference is across sessions and across mice (Wilcoxon signed-rank on per-animal medians).

      What it means

      • Maze-relative direction is the clearest non-locomotor signal. At matched speed and duration, and with head direction removed, the population still separates runs toward dead ends from runs away from them. Because head direction in the lab frame is controlled, this is direction relative to the maze's structure (toward the end of a branch vs back toward the junctions), not compass heading.
      • No evidence for planning or episodic trip memory at this resolution. Pre-departure activity, trip origin, trip destination, distance to go and time since a dead end was last visited were either not decodable or explained by posture, travel direction or immediate re-entries.
      • The behaviour is not memory-guided at the trip level. Mice reached dead ends they had not recently visited at the rate expected of a random walk, in light and in dark, and far below a walker that never reverses. A neural planning signal would have little behavioural choice to predict.

      Limitations

      • About 20 cells per session, 9.6 Hz imaging and calcium-derived activity: brief or sparse signals (e.g. sequences within a second) are below what these data can resolve.
      • The maze is small (23 cells) and trips are short; strata needed for trip-origin decoding had too few samples in most sessions.
      • Light and dark analyses each use half the data, so a null in one condition is weaker evidence than a null in pooled data.
      • Many analyses were tried; results are reported with their controls, but the surviving result should be confirmed in new data.

      Methods

      Code, statistics and references

      Population decoders: hm2p.analysis.population_maze (runner popdec); memory and planning decoders: hm2p.analysis.maze_memory (runner mazemem); dead-end recency: hm2p.analysis.dead_end_recency and scripts/run_dead_end_recency.py; this page: scripts/make_rsp_maze_report.py and scripts/build_rsp_maze_report_html.py. Decoders are L2-regularised logistic regressions with balanced class weights, cross-validated over five contiguous time blocks with 5 s gaps, scored by balanced accuracy, against 200 circular shifts. Partial rank correlations use rank residuals (Conover and Iman 1981).
