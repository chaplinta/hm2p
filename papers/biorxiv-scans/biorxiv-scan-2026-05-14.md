## Highly relevant papers

### 1. Retrosplenial PV and SST interneurons shape egocentric spatial precision and stability

Oh D, Yang J, Shin J, Kwag J. 2026.
"Retrosplenial PV and SST interneurons shape egocentric spatial precision and stability."
bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.10.724096v1

**Findings:** Parvalbumin (PV) and somatostatin (SST) interneurons in RSC perform
dissociable roles in egocentric spatial coding. PV interneurons were strongly modulated
by self-motion and exhibited bearing-aligned synchrony that preceded SST activation,
linking locomotion to egocentric coding precision. SST interneurons displayed weak
self-motion modulation but robust boundary-anchored activity with globally coherent
dynamics that stabilized spatial representations over time. Optogenetic silencing
dissociated the two: PV perturbation degraded egocentric coding precision, while SST
perturbation disrupted global population organisation without affecting local coding.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Directly relevant to interpreting interneuron contributions to
RSC spatial coding in our dataset. Although our Penk+ and Penk⁻CamKII+ populations are
excitatory pyramidal neurons (not interneurons), the interneuron circuit context matters:
PV drive sets the precision of egocentric coding, and SST provides boundary-anchored
stabilisation. This constrains how we interpret cell-type differences in HD tuning
stability — differences could arise from differential inputs from these interneuron
classes rather than intrinsic excitatory cell properties. Importantly, the SST boundary-
anchored stabilisation finding is relevant to our light/dark manipulation: if SST cells
anchor spatial codes to environmental boundaries visible under light, lights-off should
preferentially disrupt SST-mediated global population stability, which would cascade onto
downstream excitatory HD coding. This paper should be cited when discussing the local
circuit context of cell-type-specific HD tuning in the discussion.

## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 2. Spatial navigation through evolution: a single-cell atlas of the mammalian entorhinal cortex

Ralbovszki DM, Westfall JJ, Mori Y, Tahas SA, Bertelsen MF, Korshunova I, Skov J,
Gorodnik J, Hemberg M, Seemann ES, Hall V, Witter M, Khodosevich K. 2026.
"Spatial navigation through evolution: a single-cell atlas of the mammalian entorhinal
cortex." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.07.723541v1

**Findings:** Cross-species single-cell transcriptomic atlas of the entorhinal cortex from
human, Hamadryas baboon, mouse, and Egyptian fruit bat. Identified conserved principal
neuron populations and species-specific subtypes. GABAergic interneurons showed strong
conservation of SST and PV families across all four species, while VIP interneurons
exhibited pronounced species-specific divergence with an expanded repertoire in primates.
Bat-specific entorhinal subtypes associated with 3D navigation were identified.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Tangentially relevant as background for the conservation of
interneuron subtypes in parahippocampal regions across mammals. The conservation of PV
and SST interneuron identity (in contrast to the primate-expanded VIP class) provides
evolutionary context for the RSC interneuron findings in Oh et al. 2026 above. Not
directly applicable to our RSP excitatory cell-type analysis.
