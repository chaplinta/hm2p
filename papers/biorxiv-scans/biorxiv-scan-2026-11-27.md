## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 1. Prethalamus integrates neocortical and hypothalamic input to promote courage

Authors not specified in search results. 2026.
"Prethalamus integrates neocortical and hypothalamic input to promote courage."
bioRxiv. Posted 2026-11-23.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.23.768550v1

**Findings:** The ventral lateral geniculate nucleus (vLGN), an inhibitory prethalamic
structure, receives convergent input from retrosplenial cortex (RSC) and ventromedial
hypothalamus (VMH). The two inputs target largely distinct vLGN subpopulations. High
vLGN activity predicts courageous behaviour — leaving a shelter to enter a potentially
threatening open area — and optogenetic activation of vLGN promotes risk-taking across
diverse aversive conditions. RSC input to vLGN was found to promote leaving shelter,
while VMH input suppresses it.

**Relevance to hm2p:** This paper identifies RSC as an output source to the prethalamus,
specifically promoting approach/exploration in the face of threat. This is an output
pathway of RSC rather than a property of RSC HD circuitry per se. For our project it is
peripheral: our mice are freely exploring, not performing approach/avoidance. However,
the RSC→vLGN projection may carry context or spatial information (consistent with RSC's
role as a spatial context hub) and could modulate alertness during dark epochs, when the
maze environment becomes more threatening. If Penk+ and Penk⁻CamKII+ cells project
differentially to downstream targets including vLGN, this pathway could be relevant.
Not a primary citation but worth noting in the circuit anatomy section of the discussion.

### 2. Octopamine instructs head direction plasticity (revised)

Plitt MH, Turner-Evans D, Jayaraman V, Fisher YE, et al. 2025/2026.
"Octopamine instructs head direction plasticity."
bioRxiv. Original preprint posted 2025-12-11; revised posting 2026-11-24.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.24.768929v1

**Findings:** In Drosophila, octopamine-releasing neurons form a feedback loop that
carries head direction activity back to visual presynaptic terminals in the central
complex. This octopamine pathway is required for anchoring the HD ring attractor to
visual cues. Pairing activation of octopamine neurons with a visual cue is sufficient
to drive rapid plasticity — anchoring the HD signal to that cue — even without
postsynaptic HD cell activity. The result establishes neuromodulation as a gate on
visual-to-HD plasticity.

**Relevance to hm2p:** This is an insect study; direct translation to mammalian RSP is
uncertain. However, the core finding — that visual cue anchoring of HD requires a
neuromodulatory teaching signal, not just simultaneous activity — is conceptually
relevant. In our light-on epochs, noradrenaline or acetylcholine release in RSP (the
mammalian functional analogues of octopamine) could gate the degree to which visual
landmarks update the HD signal. The recently published acetylcholine–RSC preprint
(Heys et al. 2026, biorxiv 10.64898/2026.05.02.722331) showing that ACh in RSC tracks
contextual uncertainty is mechanistically consistent with this. The revision of this
preprint suggests it is approaching publication; worth monitoring for a journal version.

## Recently published journal article (not a preprint — noted for completeness)

**Retrosplenial cortex exhibits parallel directional codes and contributes to medial
entorhinal spatial coding in 3D curved-surface navigation.**
Authors (from Cell Reports record). 2026.
*Cell Reports.* Published approximately 2026-11-11.
https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(26)01375-6

This is a published journal article, not a bioRxiv preprint, so it falls outside the
strict scope of this scan. It is noted here because it is directly relevant: two-photon
calcium imaging in freely moving mice during 3D navigation found two parallel directional
codes in RSC. One code maintained coherent HD tuning across the sidewall and top platform
of a cylinder; it persisted in darkness with reduced precision. The authors also argue
that RSC activity contributes to keeping MEC positional coding stable under low visual
cue conditions. This should be added to the project's reference library and cited when
discussing RSC HD persistence in darkness.
