## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 1. Distinct medial septal GABAergic pathways differentially regulate spatial memory and theta

Authors not fully specified in search results. 2026.
"Distinct medial septal GABAergic pathways differentially regulate spatial memory and
network activity." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.01.763440v1

**Findings:** Parvalbumin-positive (PV+) and calbindin-positive (CB+) GABAergic neurons
in the medial septum project along anatomically distinct routes to downstream targets.
PV+ projections to dorsal hippocampus and medial entorhinal cortex (MEC) support
reference spatial memory. CB+ projections to entorhinal cortex support both reference
and working memory. PV+, but not CB+, neurons regulate hippocampal theta oscillations.
Theta regulation and reference memory are thus functionally dissociable within the
septo-hippocampal GABAergic system.

**Relevance to hm2p:** We use calcium imaging at ~9.6 Hz and cannot directly measure
theta. However, the finding that theta regulation is PV+-specific is relevant background
for interpreting the oscillatory context of our RSP data. Hippocampal theta drives RSC
activity and structures the timing of HD cell firing across the theta cycle; our cells
are almost certainly theta-modulated even though we cannot resolve it. The PV+ vs CB+
dissociation also provides a cell-type precedent: functionally distinct GABAergic
subpopulations can serve different aspects of spatial cognition within the same nucleus,
analogous (loosely) to our Penk+ vs Penk⁻CamKII+ dissociation in RSP excitatory cells.
Not a core citation but relevant background for the methods and discussion.
