## Near-miss: outside the 7-day window but directly relevant

### RSC interneuron cell-type dissection (posted May 11, 2026 — 25 days old)

Authors not fully specified in search results. 2026.
"Retrosplenial PV and SST interneurons shape egocentric spatial precision and stability."
bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.10.724096v1

**Findings:** PV and SST interneurons in RSC were optogenetically silenced during
spatial navigation. PV silencing degraded the precision of egocentric spatial responses
and correlated with impaired self-motion coding. SST silencing destabilised representations
globally across time without affecting moment-to-moment precision. The two cell types
support dissociable aspects of RSC spatial coding.

**Relevance to hm2p:** This paper demonstrates that genetically-defined interneuron subtypes
in RSC perform distinct computational roles — directly supporting the premise that
genetically-defined excitatory subpopulations (Penk+ vs Penk⁻CamKII+) may also show
functional dissociations. The PV/SST dissociation (precision vs stability) provides a
useful conceptual parallel: Penk+ cells may differ from non-Penk cells in one or both
dimensions. Worth citing in the introduction to frame the general logic of cell-type
dissections in RSC. This paper was outside the 7-day search window; it should have been
caught in the 2026-05-15 scan but was not — flag for inclusion in the master bibliography.
