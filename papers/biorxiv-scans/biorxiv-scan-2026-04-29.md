## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 1. Temporal dynamics of cortical state plasticity following adult vision loss

Authors not fully resolved from search results. 2026.
"Temporal Dynamics of Cortical State Plasticity Following Adult Vision Loss." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.21.719808v1
Posted: 2026-04-26. (DOI prefix 2026.04.21 reflects submission date; posted date is
April 26.) Not indexed in time for the April 28 scan.

**Findings:** Longitudinal mesoscopic (widefield) calcium imaging of dorsal cortex in
spontaneously behaving adult mice before and after bilateral enucleation. During an early
window spanning day 1 to week 3–5 post-enucleation, locomotion suppressed rather than
enhanced activity in visual and retrosplenial cortices — a reversal of the normal
locomotion-gating response. The authors describe this as a rapid, behaviour-dependent,
long-lasting reorganisation of cortical network structure, with distinct temporal windows
for different cortical areas suggesting multiple underlying plasticity mechanisms.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Tangential. The finding that RSC's locomotion-gating response is
inverted after permanent vision loss demonstrates that RSC integrates visual and
motor-state signals, and that this integration depends on intact visual input. In our
experiment, lights-off epochs are brief (1 min) and transient — far too short to induce
the long-timescale plasticity described here. However, the paper is useful background
for two reasons. First, it reinforces that RSC activity is modulated by the interaction
of locomotion state and visual input availability, which is exactly the experimental
dissociation our light/dark paradigm creates. Second, it provides a reference for the
direction of normal RSC locomotion-gating: locomotion normally enhances RSC activity; any
suppression we observe in our lights-off calcium traces is not artefactual. The study
uses widefield mesoscopic imaging rather than two-photon single-cell resolution, so
cell-type specificity is not assessed. Worth citing in the discussion of RSC's visual
input dependence.
