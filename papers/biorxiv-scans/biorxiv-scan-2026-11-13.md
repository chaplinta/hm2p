## Moderately relevant papers

### 1. Visual capture of the head-direction signal in a rat model of Fragile X Syndrome

Duszkiewicz AJ, Rastedt D, Vadher M, Wood ER, Peyrache A, Dudchenko PA. 2026.
"Visual capture of the head-direction signal in a rat model of Fragile X Syndrome."
bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.06.764867v1

Posted 2026-11-09. Missed by prior scans due to search-index lag; included here as
backfill.

**Findings:** Recorded from postsubicular (PoSub) neurons in Fmr1-/y rats (Fragile X
model) and wild-type controls during repeated 90° rotations of a single visual landmark.
In wild-type rats, the HD signal only partially followed landmark rotations, consistent
with weighted combination of visual landmark anchoring and self-motion (AHV) integration.
In Fmr1-/y rats, the visual landmark exerted stronger control — the HD signal followed
rotations more completely ("enhanced visual capture"). Enhanced visual capture was
accompanied by reduced angular head velocity (AHV) tuning strength in PoSub neurons,
suggesting that the balance between visual and self-motion inputs is shifted toward vision
when AHV integration is weakened. A related companion paper from the same group, on
altered developmental HD profiles in this model, was published simultaneously in
Communications Biology (not a preprint; not reviewed here).

**Relevance to hm2p:** This paper directly addresses the visual/self-motion balance in
the HD system, which is the core question we probe with the light/dark manipulation.
The finding that reduced AHV tuning predicts enhanced visual capture provides a
mechanistic framework: if Penk+ RSP cells preferentially rely on visual-landmark HD
signals (as we hypothesise), they might show comparatively weaker AHV tuning relative to
Penk⁻CamKII+ cells. PoSub is a primary relay of the HD signal to RSP — changes in PoSub
visual capture propagate directly into RSP inputs. The quantitative measure used here
(fraction of landmark rotation followed by the HD population vector) is a clean metric we
could apply to our data: compare how the Penk+ and Penk⁻CamKII+ population vectors
respond during the light→dark and dark→light transitions. The Fmr1-/y model is not our
system, but the mechanistic logic (visual/AHV balance sets visual capture magnitude) is
circuit-general and directly motivates an AHV tuning analysis stratified by cell type.

The week was dominated by the Zhang et al. Cell Reports publication on RSC parallel
directional codes in 3D curved-surface navigation (covered 2026-11-12.md). The
Duszkiewicz et al. PoSub preprint adds a complementary mechanistic angle: enhanced
visual capture of the HD signal co-occurs with weakened AHV tuning. This is directly
relevant to interpreting any cell-type differences in our light/dark data — if Penk+
cells show weaker AHV tuning than Penk⁻CamKII+ cells, the Duszkiewicz et al. framework
predicts they should also show stronger visual anchoring in light and larger PD drift
in darkness.

**Persistent gap:** No group has published a preprint or paper characterising
genetically-defined RSP subpopulations (Penk+, Cre-ON or Cre-OFF) in an HD or spatial
navigation context. The Zhang et al. Cell Reports paper uses bulk calcium imaging with
no cell-type identity. This gap is unchanged across all 2026 scans.

**New action item from Duszkiewicz et al.:** Add AHV tuning analysis to the cell-type
comparison. Measure AHV tuning strength (mean vector length or tuning curve width)
separately for Penk+ and Penk⁻CamKII+ cells. If Penk+ cells show weaker AHV tuning,
the visual capture framework predicts stronger light-on HD anchoring and larger
dark-epoch PD drift in that population — both are directly testable from existing
sync.h5 data.
