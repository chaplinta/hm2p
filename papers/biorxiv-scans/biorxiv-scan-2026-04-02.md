## Highly relevant papers

### 1. Dynamic coupling between visual landmark processing in area 29e and parahippocampal path integration circuits

Secer G, Krishnan B, Cowan N, Knierim JJ. 2025.
"Visual landmark coding and dynamic coupling with path integration circuits in area 29e
of the rodent parahippocampal cortex." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.11.687899v2

**Findings:** Area 29e (putative primate prostriata homologue) maintained landmark-coupled
firing even when MEC and other parahippocampal regions decoupled from landmarks. This
decoupling was preceded by a decline in gamma-band influence from 29e to MEC and reduced
theta-modulated feedback from MEC to 29e. Area 29e showed strong egocentric HD tuning
and enhanced landmark contrast sensitivity.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Directly addresses the circuit mechanism by which RSP/parahippocampal
cortex anchors spatial representations to visual landmarks. The light-off condition in
our paradigm should disrupt exactly this landmark-to-HD coupling. If Penk+ neurons are
enriched in area 29e-like function (landmark anchoring), they should show greater tuning
degradation in darkness. The gamma-band coupling result suggests a frequency-specific
mechanism we cannot access with calcium imaging, but the functional prediction (landmark
dependence) is testable.

### 2. Divergent spatial codes in RSC and hippocampus in multi-room environments

Laurent C, El Mahmoudi N, Smith DM, Sargolini F, Jacob PY. 2025.
"Divergent spatial codes in retrosplenial cortex and hippocampus support multi-scale
representation of complex environments." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.22.609122v2 (v2 updated 2025)

**Findings:** Two types of directional coding coexist in RSC: classical HD cells with
stable preferred direction across rooms, and "multidirectional" cells with room-specific
directional tuning following the geometric structure. RSC and hippocampus showed divergent
coding strategies for multi-scale spatial representation.

**Relevance to hm2p:** This is from Jacob's group and directly relevant. The
multidirectional cell type could exist in our rose maze (which has distinct arms with
different orientations). Key question: do Penk+ and non-Penk populations differ in the
proportion of classical HD vs multidirectional cells? Our maze geometry (radial arms from
a central hub) would provide a natural test — do some cells remap PD across arms? This
paper should be cited when discussing HD cell classification criteria.

### 3. Differential impact of sensory deprivation on spatial-coding cells in MEC

Tian J, Wen S, Zhou Y, Hu N, Yao S, Zhang X, Liu Y, He Y, Wang Z, Miao C. 2026.
"Differential Impact of Multiple Sensory Deprivation on Spatial-coding Cells in Medial
Entorhinal Cortex." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.09.611946v2

**Findings:** Used miniature two-photon microscopy in freely moving mice to show that
MEC head direction cells and border cells were disrupted by both light deprivation and
whisker trimming. Demonstrates that multiple sensory modalities, not just vision, contribute
to HD stability in MEC.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Methodologically close — miniature 2P in freely moving mice with
light deprivation. Their finding that MEC HD cells are disrupted by darkness provides a
direct comparison point. Our RSP data should be compared: is RSP HD tuning more or less
resilient to darkness than MEC? If RSP Penk+ cells are more visual-landmark-dependent
than MEC HD cells, that would position RSP upstream in the visual anchoring pathway.
The whisker trimming result is a reminder that our "lights off" condition eliminates
visual cues but not tactile/proprioceptive cues from maze walls.

### 4. Synchronized path-integration recalibration in HD and place cells

Jayakumar RP, Sueoka Y, Ferreyros M, Li BY, et al. 2025.
"Synchronized path-integration recalibration but distinct landmark-control dynamics in
head direction and CA1 place cells." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.08.681135v1

**Findings:** Simultaneous recording of HD cells and CA1 place cells during visual-motion
conflict. Path-integration gain recalibrated identically in both populations during forward
locomotion. However, HD cells did not recalibrate during immobility with head-scanning.
The two populations showed differential field-shifting dynamics relative to landmarks.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Directly relevant to interpreting PD drift in our dark epochs.
Their result predicts that HD recalibration depends on locomotion state — we should check
whether PD drift rate in darkness correlates with movement speed. The dissociation between
active locomotion and head-scanning is a confound to consider: mice that pause and scan in
darkness may show different drift dynamics than continuously moving mice.

### 5. Active pursuit gates egocentric coding in RSC

Authors not specified in search results. 2026.
"Active pursuit gates egocentric coding in Retrosplenial Cortex." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.14.705560v2

**Findings:** Neuropixels recordings in rat RSC during bait-chasing identified
head-centered target-encoding neurons distinct from boundary/object-coding cells.
During active pursuit, allocentric HD tuning decreased while egocentric target coding
increased. Stable environmental representations coexisted with flexible goal-related
egocentric coding.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Demonstrates that RSP HD tuning is context-dependent and
modulated by behavioural state. Our mice exploring a rose maze are not chasing bait,
but the principle applies: behavioural state (exploration vs goal-directed movement)
could modulate HD tuning differently in Penk+ vs non-Penk cells. We should check
whether HD tuning strength correlates with movement state (exploratory vs stationary).

### 6. Dissection of RSC inputs: ubiquitous drive from anterior thalamus

Margetts-Smith G, Andrianova L, Kohli S, Randall AD, Aggleton JP, Witton J, Craig MT.
2025. "Dissection of retrosplenial cortex inputs: ubiquitous drive from anterior
thalamus." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.06.636939v1

**Findings:** Combined viral tracing, optogenetic stimulation, and patch-clamp
electrophysiology to map inputs from ACC, dorsal subiculum, and anterior thalamic
nuclei (ATN) to granular and dysgranular RSC. All recorded RSC pyramidal neurons
received ATN input regardless of subdivision or cortical layer.

**Relevance to hm2p:** ATN is the primary thalamic HD input to RSC. The finding that
ATN input is ubiquitous across all RSC pyramidal neurons means both Penk+ and non-Penk
populations likely receive ATN HD signals. This constrains our interpretation: if both
populations receive the same HD input, cell-type-specific differences in HD tuning must
arise from local circuit processing or differential integration of other inputs (visual
cortex, subiculum), not from differential ATN drive. This is an important point for the
discussion.

### 7. HD cells in postsubiculum show parallax errors during visual anchoring

Authors not fully specified. 2025.
"Head-Direction Cells in Postsubiculum Show Systematic Parallax Errors During Visual
Anchoring." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.25.650191v1

**Findings:** Postsubicular HD cells show a systematic parallax bias in decoded HD
depending on viewing angle to a visual cue. The error was smaller than predicted by a
pure vision model, explained by combination of angular head velocity integration with
simple visual anchoring. The HD system does not perform explicit parallax correction.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Relevant for understanding HD decoding accuracy in our maze. The
rose maze has cues at varying distances; parallax effects could differentially affect
decoded HD depending on the mouse's position. This is a potential confound for any
position-dependent analysis of HD tuning. However, in our lights-off condition, this
effect disappears entirely, providing a clean comparison.

### 8. Learning to use landmarks amplifies their representation in RSC

Authors not fully specified. 2024.
"Learning to use landmarks for navigation amplifies their representation in
retrosplenial cortex." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.18.607457v1

**Findings:** Chronic two-photon imaging of RSC neuronal ensembles during spatial
learning showed a pronounced increase in landmark-referenced activity that, once
established, remained stable across days. Visual encoding shifted to landmark-referenced
and context-dependent codes as cues acquired spatial meaning. Uncoupling treadmill motion
from visual feedback altered responses associated with visual-motion coherence.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Demonstrates that RSC landmark representations are learned and
stable. In our dataset, mice are well-trained in the maze, so landmark representations
should be established. The visual-motion coherence finding is relevant: in our lights-off
epochs, visual flow is absent while self-motion continues — exactly the dissociation
this paper studied. The key prediction is that neurons with strong landmark coding
(possibly Penk+?) should be most affected by lights-off.

### 9. Anterior-posterior input patterns support visuospatial processing in mouse RSC

Wei, Couto, Kloosterman, Bonin. 2025.
"Specific anterior-posterior brain-wide input patterns support specialized visuospatial
processing in the mouse retrosplenial cortex." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.24.661247v1

**Findings:** Combined 2P calcium imaging in navigating mice with brain-wide retrograde
tracing. Anterior RSC neurons had sharper, more reliable position tuning and preferred
fast visual stimuli. Posterior RSC neurons showed broader tuning and preferred slower
motion. Clear anterior-posterior gradient in input patterns.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Our imaging is single-plane per session — we should document
where along the A-P axis we are imaging. If our FOV is consistently anterior or posterior
RSC, this constrains interpretation. The gradient in visual processing properties could
interact with cell-type identity: are Penk+ cells enriched at a particular A-P position?
This is a confound to address or at least acknowledge.

### 10. Subspace communication in the hippocampal-retrosplenial axis

Gonzalez J, Voroeslakos M, Aykan D, Soto N, Nitzan N, Swanson R, Chen ZS, Buzsaki G.
2026. "Subspace communication in the hippocampal-retrosplenial axis." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.31.697203v1

**Findings:** Identified neuronal subspaces that recombined overlapping neuronal pools to
support distinct interareal interactions across experiences and brain states. CA1-CA3
subspace reactivation during post-experience sleep correlated with replay; CA1-RSC
subspaces did not, reflecting a plasticity-stability balance.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Provides a framework for thinking about RSP population dynamics.
The finding that CA1-RSC subspaces are stable (not reactivated during sleep like CA1-CA3)
suggests RSP maintains a more stable representation. Relevant for population-level analyses
(CEBRA, decoding) — our Penk+ and non-Penk populations may form distinct subspaces with
different hippocampal communication patterns.

### 11. Theta activity in RSC anchors space to body cardinal axes

Authors not fully specified. 2025.
"Theta activity in the RSC anchors space to the body cardinal axes." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.02.691796v1

**Findings:** Human EEG study using VR during goal-directed rotations. RSC theta bursts
encoded both acceleration and alignment with the body's principal axes. This body-axis-
anchored signal emerged only during goal-directed rotations and correlated with navigation
performance.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Suggests RSC has body-axis-referenced coding beyond classical
allocentric HD. We cannot measure theta with calcium imaging, but the functional
prediction (body-axis alignment coding) could manifest as enhanced tuning at cardinal
body orientations. Human study, so species translation is uncertain, but worth noting
in the discussion of egocentric vs allocentric RSP coding.

## Relevant but less directly applicable

### 12. RSC enables context-dependent goal-directed sensorimotor transformation

Bech P, Dard R, et al. 2026.
"Retrosplenial cortex enables context-dependent goal-directed sensorimotor
transformation." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685738v2

RSC was the first dorsal cortical area to discriminate context during whisker-guided
sensorimotor tasks. Demonstrates RSC's role beyond spatial navigation per se. Less
directly relevant to HD tuning but relevant for framing RSC as a contextual integration
hub — our light/dark manipulation is a context switch.

## Summary of implications for hm2p

**Strengthens the project:**
- No papers characterise HD properties of genetically-defined RSP subpopulations (Penk+
  vs non-Penk). The gap remains open.
- Multiple recent papers (Secer et al., Laurent/Jacob et al., landmark learning paper)
  reinforce that RSP visual landmark anchoring is a central function, making our light/dark
  manipulation well-motivated.
- The Tian et al. 2026 MEC paper using miniature 2P with light deprivation is the closest
  methodological parallel — provides direct comparison data for MEC HD cells in darkness.

**New considerations:**
- Laurent/Jacob et al.'s "multidirectional cells" in multi-room RSC — check for these in
  our rose maze arms.
- Margetts-Smith et al.'s finding that all RSC pyramids receive ATN input constrains our
  interpretation of cell-type HD differences to local processing, not differential thalamic
  drive.
- Wei et al.'s A-P gradient in RSC function — document our imaging position.
- Jayakumar et al.'s locomotion-dependent recalibration — analyse PD drift as a function
  of movement speed in dark epochs.

**Papers to cite in the manuscript:**
