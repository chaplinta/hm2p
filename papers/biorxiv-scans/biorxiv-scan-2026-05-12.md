## Highly relevant papers

### 1. Retrosplenial PV and SST interneurons shape egocentric spatial precision and stability

Oh D, Yang J, Shin J, Kwag J. 2026.
"Retrosplenial PV and SST interneurons shape egocentric spatial precision and
stability." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.10.724096v1

**Findings:** Two-photon calcium imaging combined with cell-type-specific optogenetic
silencing in freely moving mice. Parvalbumin (PV) interneurons in RSC are strongly
modulated by self-motion and exhibit bearing-aligned population synchrony that precedes
somatostatin (SST) interneuron activation. Optogenetic suppression of PV interneurons
degraded egocentric coding precision without disrupting global population organisation.
Suppression of SST interneurons had the opposite profile: robust boundary-anchored
activity was disrupted and global population coherence collapsed, while moment-to-moment
egocentric precision was preserved. The two interneuron subtypes thus regulate
dissociable components of RSC spatial representation.

**Relevance to hm2p:** This is the most relevant RSC preprint in the scan series since
the April 2 batch. Our Penk+ and Penk⁻CamKII+ populations are excitatory pyramidal
neurons, but PV and SST interneurons provide the inhibitory scaffold that controls
their output. The dissociation between PV-gated egocentric precision and SST-gated
population stability provides a local-circuit interpretation for any cell-type difference
we observe between Penk+ and non-Penk neurons. Specifically: if Penk+ neurons show
lower HD tuning precision but more stable preferred-direction (PD) identity across
epochs, this would be consistent with preferential SST-circuit engagement; if they
show sharper instantaneous tuning but higher PD drift, a PV-circuit interpretation
would follow. The paper also uses freely moving mice with two-photon imaging — the
same experimental configuration as hm2p. The lights-off condition in our paradigm
removes visual cues that SST-anchored boundary coding depends on; we predict SST
targets (potentially Penk+ neurons?) should degrade more in darkness than PV targets.
This paper should be cited in the Methods section (animal preparation and two-photon
approach) and in the Discussion when interpreting differential HD tuning between
cell types.

## Moderately relevant papers

### 2. Spatial navigation through evolution: a single-cell atlas of the mammalian entorhinal cortex

Ralbovszki DM, Westfall JJ, Mori Y, Tahas SA, Bertelsen MF, Korshunova I, Skov J,
Gorodkin J, Hemberg M, Seemann ES, Hall V, Witter M, Khodosevich K. 2026.
"Spatial navigation through evolution: a single-cell atlas of the mammalian entorhinal
cortex." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.07.723541v1

**Findings:** Cross-species single-cell RNA-seq atlas of entorhinal cortex (EC) from
mouse, Hamadryas baboon, Egyptian fruit bat, and human. Identified conserved excitatory
neuron populations across species, including mixed-layer identities and bat-specific
subtypes. Inhibitory interneurons showed strong conservation of SST and PV families
across all species, with VIP GABAergic neurons showing pronounced primate-specific
expansion. The atlas provides a molecular taxonomy of EC cell types, including the
layer II stellate cells and layer III pyramidal neurons that give rise to grid cell and
border cell computation. The bat — a 3D navigator — provided an outgroup to test
which cell types are conserved by navigational demand versus those that diverged with
cortical expansion.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Not directly about RSP and not about HD cells per se, but
provides two pieces of context. First, the molecular cell-type conservation data
strengthen the expectation that functional cell types identified in mice (including
our Penk+ RSP neurons) have conserved molecular identities rather than being rodent
artefacts. Second, the finding that SST and PV interneuron families are deeply
conserved in EC (and by implication throughout allocortex) is consistent with
paper 1 above: the local inhibitory circuit framework identified in RSC by Oh et al.
is likely to be a conserved feature across navigational regions. The atlas also
documents the EC molecular context that sends grid cell and HD inputs to RSC —
any cell-type-specific projection from EC layer III to RSP can now be cross-referenced
to this atlas for molecular identity.
