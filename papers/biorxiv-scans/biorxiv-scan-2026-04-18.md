## Moderately relevant papers

### 1. Path Integration and Spatial Updating Recruit Distinct Cognitive-Neural Mechanisms in Humans

Authors not fully specified in search results. 2026.
"Path Integration and Spatial Updating Recruit Distinct Cognitive-Neural Mechanisms
in Humans." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.11.717901v1

Posted: 2026-04-14.

**Findings:** Human fMRI study dissociating path integration (continuous self-motion
integration) from spatial updating (discrete positional updating on landmark
encounters). The two processes recruited distinct cortical and subcortical networks,
with path integration dependent on posterior parietal / entorhinal circuits and spatial
updating on hippocampal-RSC circuits.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Provides a conceptual framework for interpreting PD drift in our
dark epochs. In darkness, mice rely on path integration without landmark-driven spatial
updating; any PD drift is attributable to path integration accumulation error. The
dissociation supports our experimental design: lights-off selectively removes the spatial
updating component while preserving idiothetic path integration. Human fMRI limits direct
translation, but the RSC involvement in spatial updating maps onto our population. Confirms
the conceptual motivation for the light/dark manipulation.

## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 2. Silent hippocampal engrams shape future memory processing

Authors not fully specified in search results. 2026.
"Silent hippocampal engrams shape future memory processing." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.12.717978v1

Posted: 2026-04-12.

**Findings:** Silent (non-reactivated) hippocampal engram cells formed during prior
experiences constrain the encoding of subsequent experiences by reducing overlap between
new and old cell assemblies. Engram silencing promoted pattern separation for overlapping
spatial contexts.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Tangential — concerns hippocampal memory encoding, not RSP HD
tuning. However, relevant background if we interpret session-to-session stability of RSP
HD representations: if RSP representations are partly hippocampus-dependent, engram
dynamics could explain inter-session drift or remapping. Not a priority citation.

## Notable journal publication (not a preprint)

### 3. Anterior and posterior retrosplenial cortex form distinct visuospatial circuits in the mouse

Wei YT, Couto J, Kloosterman F, Bonin V. 2026.
"Anterior and posterior retrosplenial cortex form distinct visuospatial circuits in
the mouse." Nature Communications.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70762-z

**Note:** This is the peer-reviewed publication of the bioRxiv preprint "Specific
anterior-posterior brain-wide input patterns support specialized visuospatial processing
in the mouse retrosplenial cortex" (Wei et al. 2025, bioRxiv 2025.06.24.661247), which
was noted in the 2026-04-02 scan. The paper has now been formally published in Nature
Communications. The bioRxiv version has been confirmed and peer-reviewed.

**Findings (confirmed from published version):** Combined two-photon calcium imaging in
navigating mice with brain-wide retrograde tracing. Anterior RSC neurons show sharper,
more reliable position tuning and prefer fast, low–spatial-frequency visual motion.
Posterior RSC neurons show broader position selectivity, stronger responses to slow,
high–spatial-frequency visual patterns, and enhanced tuning in visually immersive
environments. Clear anterior–posterior gradient in both functional properties and
long-range connectivity patterns.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Directly relevant — now formally published and citable. We must
document where along the A-P axis our imaging plane sits in each session. If our FOV
systematically samples one subregion, this published gradient constrains interpretation
of any differences between Penk+ and Penk⁻CamKII+ cells. The visual motion tuning
difference (fast vs slow) could interact with our light/dark manipulation in a
subregion-dependent way. Cite this paper in the methods and discussion.
