## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 1. ClearGel enables chronic near-millimetre-depth two-photon imaging through the intact skull

Authors not fully specified in search results. 2026.
"ClearGel: a thermoresponsive hydrogel for chronic, near-millimeter two-photon imaging
through the intact skull." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.04.764111v1

**Findings:** ClearGel is a thermoreversible hydrogel applied to the intact skull to
optically clear bone and preserve mechanical integrity. It supports awake functional
two-photon imaging of GCaMP-expressing neurons at depths not previously achievable
through the skull without craniotomy. The authors demonstrate single-cell calcium
imaging and report that chronic preparations remain stable across weeks. Relevant
biomechanical and optical characterisation data are provided.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Our preparation uses a cranial window over RSP — this paper is
background for the methods discussion contrasting intact-skull vs craniotomy approaches.
The gel chemistry is not directly applicable to head-mounted 2P (ClearGel is designed
for benchtop upright scopes), but the depth and stability claims are useful context for
comparing imaging quality across preparation types. File as a methods note in the
discussion of imaging preparation limitations.

### 2. Curved skull geometry shapes the observability of hippocampal oscillations

Authors not fully specified in search results. 2026.
"Curved geometry shapes the observability of hippocampal oscillations." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.05.764490v1

**Findings:** Computational modelling shows that the curved laminar geometry of the
hippocampus acts as a spatial filter for coherent theta activity. Depending on electrode
placement relative to the curvature, classical theta rhythms may be observed, attenuated,
or entirely cancelled in LFP recordings. The model predicts systematic recording-site
biases in estimated theta power and coherence.

**Relevance to hm2p:** We do not record LFP and therefore are not directly affected by
this finding. However, it is relevant background for interpreting the hippocampal
theta literature that contextualises our calcium imaging data. Reports of theta
modulation in RSC and HD cells often rely on LFP-based theta estimates that could be
subject to the geometric biases described here. This is a note of caution when citing
theta-dependent RSC data from LFP studies, not a core citation.
