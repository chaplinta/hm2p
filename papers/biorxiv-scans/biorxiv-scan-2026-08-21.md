### 1. Structured navigation in a goal-directed task reveals flexible spatial coding

Authors not yet specified in indexed results. 2026.
"Structured navigation in a goal-directed task reveals flexible spatial coding." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.27.741044v1

Posted: 2026-07-27 (25 days before this scan; not within the 7-day window but not in
any prior scan).

**Findings:** Introduces a platform combining freely moving behaviour with precise
environmental control (trajectory, reward location, visual stimuli projection) compatible
with chronic Neuropixels recordings and real-time pose estimation. Recordings in medial
entorhinal cortex revealed canonical spatial and head direction tuning, alongside
population-level activity tracking distance to task-relevant goals. Addresses the tension
between behavioural richness and experimental control that constrains most freely-moving
navigation studies.

**Relevance to hm2p:** The platform design is highly relevant — it achieves free movement
with precise cue control, which is close to what our light/dark paradigm attempts (cue
removal rather than cue control). The MEC HD tuning data provides a comparison point for
RSP HD tuning under equivalent recording conditions. If the Giocomo lab extends this to
RSP, it would be a direct competitor. Worth watching for follow-up submissions.

### 2. Retrosplenial PV and SST interneurons shape egocentric spatial precision and stability

Oh D, Yang J, Shin J, Kwag J. 2026.
"Retrosplenial PV and SST interneurons shape egocentric spatial precision and stability."
bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.10.724096v1

Posted: 2026-05-10 (not in earlier scans; may have been missed).

**Findings:** Used optogenetic silencing of parvalbumin (PV) and somatostatin (SST)
interneurons in RSC during navigation. PV interneurons are strongly modulated by
self-motion and show bearing-aligned synchrony that precedes SST activation, linking
movement to egocentric coding precision. SST interneurons show weak self-motion modulation
but robust boundary-anchored activity with globally coherent dynamics that stabilise
spatial representations over time. Silencing PV degraded egocentric coding precision;
silencing SST disrupted global population organisation.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Directly relevant to the circuit context of our Penk+ and
Penk⁻CamKII+ populations. Penk+ neurons in RSP are glutamatergic pyramidal cells — this
paper characterises the interneuron inhibitory framework within which they operate. The
PV/SST dissociation (movement-linked precision vs. boundary-anchored stability) is a
potential mechanism by which local inhibition shapes HD tuning in each cell-type population.
The self-motion modulation of PV interneurons is particularly relevant: in our dark
epochs, visual input is removed but self-motion signals persist — PV-mediated inhibition
could be the channel through which path-integration signals reach pyramidal cells in
darkness. Should be cited in the discussion of interneuron contributions to HD stability.

### A. Anterior and posterior RSC employ distinct egocentric–allocentric transformation strategies

Full citation pending (direct fetch blocked).
"Anterior and posterior retrosplenial cortex employ distinct strategies for
egocentric–allocentric transformation in spatial coding." PNAS. 2026.
doi:10.1073/pnas.2600565123

**Findings (from search abstract):** Two-photon calcium imaging in freely navigating mice.
Anterior RSC predominantly encodes egocentric boundary vectors (EBV); posterior RSC
integrates egocentric information with head direction to produce sharpened allocentric
boundary representations. A specific RSC→MEC subpopulation generates more abstract global
allocentric representations enriching downstream navigation circuits.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Directly constrains interpretation of our data. If Penk+ and
Penk⁻CamKII+ neurons are not uniformly distributed along the A–P axis of RSP, the
egocentric/allocentric distinction could confound cell-type comparisons. The RSC→MEC
projection subtype result suggests a functional projection-specific subpopulation within
RSP pyramidal neurons — Penk identity and projection target may co-segregate. We should
document our imaging position along the A–P axis and check whether it falls in the
region characterised here as predominantly anterior (EBV) or posterior (HD-integrated
allocentric).

### B. Diversity and sensorimotor specialization of HD cells in mouse thalamus

Hijazi, Jiang, et al. 2026.
"Diversity and sensorimotor specialization of head direction cells in the mouse thalamus."
Current Biology. doi listed in source (s0960-9822(26)00631-7).

**Findings (from search abstract):** Identifies distinct HD cell subpopulations in the
mouse anterodorsal thalamic nucleus (ADn) based on firing patterns, connectivity, and
neurochemical profile including calretinin immunoreactivity. Subpopulations differ in
sensorimotor modulation.

**Relevance to hm2p:** ADn is the primary thalamic HD input to RSP. If ADn HD cells are
heterogeneous, RSP might receive functionally distinct HD signals from different ADn
subpopulations — potentially explaining differential HD tuning strength across Penk+ vs
Penk⁻CamKII+ cells even if, as Margetts-Smith et al. (2025) showed, all RSP pyramids
receive *some* ATN input. The calretinin-defined subtype is worth cross-referencing with
projection mapping studies.
