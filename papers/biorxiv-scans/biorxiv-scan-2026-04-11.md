## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 1. A multiplexed striatal architecture for generalised spatial goal progress

Authors not fully specified. 2026.
"A multiplexed striatal architecture for generalized spatial goal progress." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.08.716650v1

Posted: April 8, 2026.

**Findings:** Large-scale population recordings and causal perturbations in rats
navigating toward dynamically changing goals in multiple maze geometries. Nucleus
accumbens (NAc) activity was found to encode a scale-invariant, normalised
distance-to-goal signal that generalised across maze shapes and task rules, identifying
a multiplexed striatal architecture for tracking spatial goal progress.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Not directly relevant to RSP HD cell-type comparisons. However,
the result describes a striatal goal-distance signal that could interact with RSP spatial
coding during goal-directed navigation. Our rose maze mice are not performing a goal-directed
task (they explore freely), but any goal-directed sub-epochs (e.g., returning to a familiar
arm) could engage this system. The main interest is as background: if NAc encodes
goal-progress and RSP encodes HD, understanding how these interact during navigation is
a future direction. Does not require action in the current analysis.

### 2. A theory of subicular function and generalised vector coding

Authors not fully specified. 2026.
"A theory of subicular function and generalized vector coding." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.04.716474v1

Posted: April 4, 2026. (May have been captured in the April 4 scan; included here for
completeness if not.)

**Findings:** Proposes Disco, a theoretical framework positing that subicular activity
signals discontinuities in the flow of experience, coded in a vector basis. Geometric
discontinuities (walls, drops, objects, corners, curved surfaces) drive canonical
boundary/object responses. Non-spatial discontinuities in behavioural state yield
axis-coding. The framework unifies many seemingly disparate subicular cell types
(boundary vector cells, object vector cells, goal-direction cells, splitter cells)
under a single principle.

**Relevance to hm2p:** The subiculum is a major input to RSP via the PoS→RSP pathway
and is one synapse upstream of the HD cells we record. If subicular coding organises as
vector discontinuities, the spatial structure of HD inputs reaching RSP may be shaped by
maze geometry (arm boundaries, junctions). This could manifest as position-dependent
modulation of RSP HD tuning that differs between open corridors and arm junctions in our
rose maze — a confound worth checking. The framework is theoretical; no direct
experimental comparison to our data, but relevant for discussion of the RSP input
landscape.
