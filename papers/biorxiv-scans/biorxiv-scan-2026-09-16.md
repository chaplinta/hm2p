## Published note

**Newly identified peer-reviewed paper not previously covered in any scan:**

Yang Y, Zhang X, Yuan X, Cai H, Cao Q, Miao C, et al. 2026.
"Anterior and posterior retrosplenial cortex employ distinct strategies for
egocentric–allocentric transformation in spatial coding."
*PNAS* 123(31):e2600565123.
https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2600565123
Published 2026-07-28 (online); 2026-08-04 (issue). Received 2026-01-08; accepted
2026-06-24. No prior bioRxiv preprint found.

**Findings:** Used two-photon calcium imaging in freely navigating mice to characterise
functional differences along the anterior–posterior axis of RSC. The anterior RSC (aRSC)
was predominantly characterised by egocentric boundary vector (EBV) coding. The
posterior RSC (pRSC) exhibited enhanced allocentric boundary representation, integrating
egocentric inputs with head-direction signals to construct sharpened allocentric
boundary representations. Neurons projecting to MEC displayed the most abstract,
globally coherent allocentric codes, suggesting a hierarchical processing cascade:
aRSC (egocentric input) → pRSC (egocentric-to-allocentric transformation) → MEC
projection neurons (abstract allocentric).

**Relevance to hm2p:** Highly relevant, particularly as a complement to Wei et al. 2026
(Nature Communications, covered in Sept 14 scan) which also described an A-P RSC
gradient but focused on visuospatial circuits and input patterns. Yang et al. now
establish the *functional* axis: egocentric BEV cells are enriched anteriorly, allocentric
cells posteriorly. For hm2p, the critical question is where along this axis our imaging
plane sits. If our FOV is predominantly posterior RSC (pRSC), the cells we record are
more likely to carry allocentric HD signals that depend on integrating head-direction
with visual landmarks — meaning lights-off should preferentially disrupt cells in our
imaging plane. If anterior RSC, egocentric boundary cells (wall-following, tactile)
should dominate and persist in darkness. This is a testable interpretation of the A-P
confound flagged in previous scans. Should be cited alongside Wei et al. 2026 when
discussing imaging plane position and spatial coding gradients.
