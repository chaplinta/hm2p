## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 1. Multimodal profiling and intersectional genetics reveal CA1 border inhibitory neuron diversity and postnatal emergence of retrosplenial connectivity

Authors not specified in search snippets. 2026.
"Multimodal profiling and intersectional genetics reveal CA1 border inhibitory neuron
diversity and postnatal emergence of retrosplenial connectivity." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.29.762784v1

Posted: 29 October 2026.

**Findings:** Combines multimodal transcriptomic profiling, intersectional genetics,
and retrograde tracing to characterise inhibitory neurons at the CA1
stratum-radiatum/lacunosum-moleculare border. A new Cxcl14-Cre mouse line, combined
with an interneuron-specific viral enhancer, provides genetic access to this population.
Retrograde labelling links the RSP-projecting subpopulation to Ntng1 expression. The
long-range CA1→RSP inhibitory projection emerges postnatally (between days P3 and P5).

**Relevance to hm2p:** This is circuit anatomy rather than function, and not about HD
tuning or spatial navigation. However, long-range hippocampal inhibitory inputs to RSP
are relevant background for interpreting hippocampal-RSP interactions in our dataset.
A CA1 inhibitory projection that targets RSP apical tufts could modulate RSP HD cell
activity during periods of active navigation — particularly relevant for understanding
why RSP HD tuning might be episodic or state-dependent. The Cxcl14-Cre tool is useful
for future circuit-level experiments dissecting which RSP cell types (Penk+ vs
Penk⁻CamKII+) receive this inhibitory input.

### 2. Direct cortical feedforward and feedback pathways differentially shape visual responses and selectively engage looped circuits

Vasile F, Oude Lohuis MN, et al. (Petreanu lab). 2026.
"Direct cortical feedforward and feedback pathways differentially shape visual responses
and selectively engage looped circuits." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.27.762146v1

Posted: 27 October 2026. (Missed by the Oct 28 scan, which covered Oct 21–28 but did
not return this paper.)

**Findings:** Uses retrograde labelling, multi-area two-photon imaging, and optogenetics
to examine feedforward (FF) and feedback (FB) pathways between mouse primary visual
cortex (V1), posteromedial cortex (PM), and retrosplenial cortex (RSP). FF inputs
produce additive response changes; FB inputs produce multiplicative modulation and
improved visual decoding performance. The two pathway types engage distinct looped
circuit motifs.

**Relevance to hm2p:** RSP is included as a target region in the multi-area imaging
dataset. The finding that FB signals into RSP are multiplicative (gain-modulating rather
than additive) is relevant background for interpreting how visual information reaches
RSP during our light-on epochs. If visual landmark signals arrive in RSP via FB from
PM/V1, they would act as a gain modulator on ongoing HD-related activity rather than
driving RSP neurons directly. This could explain why RSP HD tuning sharpens rather
than shifts in the light — a testable prediction for light vs dark epoch comparison
in our data. Not about HD cells, not about freely moving mice; the optogenetic
stimulation was done in head-fixed preparation.
