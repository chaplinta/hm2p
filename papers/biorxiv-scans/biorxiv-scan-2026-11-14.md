## Highly relevant papers

### 1. Visual capture of the head-direction signal in a rat model of Fragile X Syndrome

Duszkiewicz AJ, Råstedt AA, Vadher A, Wood ER, Peyrache A, Dudchenko PA. 2026.
"Visual capture of the head-direction signal in a rat model of Fragile X Syndrome."
bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.06.764867v1

**Findings:** In wild-type rats, the head-direction signal only partially tracked landmark
rotations — consistent with weighted integration of visual and self-motion (path
integration) information — and visual landmark control weakened progressively across
repeated cue conflicts, indicating experience-dependent recalibration of visual weighting.
In Fmr1⁻/y (Fragile X) rats, the HD signal closely followed landmark rotations regardless
of cue conflict history, with no experience-dependent recalibration. This builds on a
companion preprint from the same group (Duszkiewicz et al. 2025, bioRxiv 2025.01.09.632139)
showing altered developmental HD profiles in Fmr1⁻/y rats; the present preprint extends
that work to adult animals and directly manipulates visual cue control. Recording site
appears to be postsubiculum (PoSub) based on the companion paper; reduced angular head
velocity tuning in PoSub neurons of Fmr1⁻/y rats was noted in the search results.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Directly addresses the balance between visual and path-integration
(idiothetic) inputs to the HD system — the core question our light/dark paradigm tests.
The partial visual following in WT provides a quantitative framework: rather than a binary
visual vs. path-integration split, the HD system weights both and updates those weights with
experience. This is the mechanistic basis for drift in darkness (path integration accumulates
error without visual recalibration) and for rapid re-anchoring when lights return.

For hm2p specifically: (1) If Penk+ neurons are preferentially driven by visual input, they
should show stronger visual capture under lights-on and faster realignment at lights-on onset
after a dark epoch — analogous to the over-capture phenotype in Fmr1⁻/y rats, but as a
cell-type property rather than a pathological state. (2) The experience-dependent weakening
of visual control in WT suggests our mice may show less tight visual anchoring in later
repeats of the light-on epochs within a session — worth checking across epochs 1→6. (3) The
PoSub angular velocity tuning reduction in Fmr1⁻/y rats suggests that stronger visual
anchoring comes at the cost of weaker path-integration input encoding — a potential
prediction for Penk+ cells if they are more visually driven.

## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 2. A protocol for cortical and deep-brain calcium imaging in freely moving mice with the Mini2P system

Tello Garcia I, Alhassen W, et al. 2026.
"A protocol for cortical and deep-brain calcium imaging in freely moving mice with the
Mini2P system." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.09.764980v1

**Findings:** Step-by-step protocol for head-mounted Mini2P calcium imaging in freely
moving mice. Covers system preparation and performance checks, optical alignment, field-of-
view selection, baseplate installation, microscope attachment/reattachment procedures,
behavioral habituation, task integration, acquisition quality control, and troubleshooting.
Explicitly designed to be adaptable across brain regions and behavioral paradigms.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Our imaging uses an earlier-generation head-mounted 2P system. This
protocol is a useful reference for imaging procedure documentation (Methods section) and
for troubleshooting guidance. The habituation and baseplate procedures are transferable. Not
scientifically novel relative to the original MINI2P paper (Zong et al. 2022, Cell) but
useful as a citable methods reference for the freely-moving 2P approach.

### 3. Amyloidosis and tauopathy progressively disrupt near-critical dynamics in selectively vulnerable circuits

Authors not fully specified in search results. 2026.
"Amyloidosis and tauopathy progressively disrupt near-critical dynamics in selectively
vulnerable circuits." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.11.766020v1

**Findings:** Single-unit recordings in retrosplenial cortex, anterior cingulate cortex,
CA1 hippocampus, and primary visual cortex of freely behaving young and old APP/PS1 and
wild-type mice. Both APP/PS1 (amyloid) and P301S/E4 (tauopathy) models showed progressive
displacement from near-critical network dynamics in RSC and ACC, with CA1 showing the same
effect in the tauopathy model. The authors argue that molecularly distinct pathologies
converge on loss of criticality in selectively vulnerable circuits.

**Relevance to hm2p:** RSC is one of the recorded brain areas, confirming that RSC single-
unit activity in freely behaving mice is tractable. The methods may be of interest for
comparison (Neuropixels vs. our 2P GCaMP approach). The science is not relevant to our
project — we are studying healthy, young mice and HD tuning — but the paper reinforces that
RSC is a selectively vulnerable area with distinctive functional properties worth
characterising in healthy animals. Not citable in our manuscript.

## Summary

**Papers found within the 7-day window:** 3 (1 highly relevant, 2 tangential/methods).

**Most important finding this week:** Duszkiewicz et al. 2026 (item 1) is the standout
paper. It provides the clearest mechanistic framing to date for the visual vs. idiothetic
weighting question that underpins our light/dark paradigm. The key analytical implication
is that visual capture should be measured as a continuous weighting parameter (how much
does the HD signal follow a cue rotation or fail to drift in darkness?) rather than as
a binary switch. This framing should shape how we quantify the lights-off effect in our
Penk+ and non-Penk populations.

**Notable trend:** Relatively quiet week for RSC and HD literature. The bioRxiv search
engine may lag a few days on the most recent postings, so one or two relevant papers
posted Nov 13–14 may not yet be indexed.
