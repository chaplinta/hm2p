## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 1. Hippocampal representations differentiate reactive and anticipatory responses during foraging under threat

Authors not fully specified in search results. 2026.
"Hippocampal representations differentiate reactive and anticipatory responses during
foraging under threat." bioRxiv.
URL not recovered — appeared in a dated listing for April 17, 2026, but no stable
link was returned by any search. Search for author names or title for the direct URL.

Posted: approximately April 17, 2026.

**Findings:** Partial search summary only. The preprint examined hippocampal population
dynamics during foraging in the presence of threat, distinguishing reactive neural
responses (triggered by threat onset) from anticipatory signals (predictive coding before
threat exposure). The study appears to use calcium imaging in freely behaving rodents
during a foraging task with periodic threat epochs.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Tangentially relevant. We image during light/dark alternation —
a context switch analogous in some respects to a predictable threat epoch. If the
hippocampal anticipatory coding described here has a retrosplenial correlate, it could
manifest in our data as differences in HD tuning quality near light-transition times
(the first few seconds of a dark epoch, when mice may show anticipatory changes). This
is speculative given we only have a partial summary; retrieve the paper before acting
on this. The study is hippocampal, not RSP, so it is not directly relevant to our
cell-type comparison.
