## Moderately relevant papers

### 1. Topographic CA1 input shapes subicular spatial coding

Authors not fully specified. 2026.
"Topographic CA1 input shapes subicular spatial coding." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.24.714092v1

Posted: ~March 24–26, 2026. Not covered in any previous scan.

**Findings:** Used Latrophilin-2 conditional knockout mice to selectively disrupt
CA1-to-subiculum topographic projections. Precise CA1–subiculum topography shapes
the anatomical distribution of subicular spatial coding while preserving single-cell
tuning properties in isolation. Disrupting topography selectively impairs boundary
vector coding and long-term network stability. The results identify CA1 afferent
topography as an indispensable scaffold for organising subicular spatial maps.

**Relevance to hm2p:** The subiculum is a major intermediate in the hippocampus →
RSP input pathway and is strongly implicated in HD circuit function (PoS/subiculum
projects to RSP). This paper shows that the spatial content of subicular output is
shaped by the topographic organisation of CA1 input — not just by intrinsic subicular
properties. If CA1 topographic input shapes the HD signals leaving the subiculum, then
the HD representation arriving in RSP carries a structured spatial scaffold inherited
from the CA1–subiculum projection pattern. This provides additional motivation to
document our imaging position along the RSP A–P axis: spatial content in subicular
inputs (and therefore RSP inputs) varies topographically. The selective impairment of
boundary vector coding by topographic disruption is also relevant — border cells in
RSP (which we may encounter) may receive boundary information from subiculum, and
disruption of this pathway would selectively impair border but not HD coding. Consistent
with the Margetts-Smith et al. 2025 finding (April 2 scan) that ATN input to RSP is
ubiquitous — subiculum provides a complementary, topographically organised spatial input.

## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 2. Can grid cells produce hexadirectional signals?

Authors not fully specified. 2026.
"Can grid cells produce hexadirectional signals?" bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.04.709667v1

Posted: ~March 4–7, 2026. Not covered in any previous scan.

**Findings:** Evaluated whether hexadirectional analysis — a method used to infer
grid-cell population activity from fMRI BOLD and single-unit LFP signals in humans
and freely moving rats — actually reflects grid-cell firing. The authors show that
hexadirectional modulation can emerge from the firing variance of MEC recordings
without requiring a true hexagonal tuning signature. Identified potential confounds
and recommended controls when interpreting hexadirectional signals as evidence for
grid-cell activity in either humans or rodents.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Methods paper; not about RSP or HD cells directly. Indirectly
relevant as a caution for interpreting any directional periodicity in our RSP
population-level analyses. If we apply population-level directional decoding or look
for angular periodicity in RSP neural responses, similar confounds could apply. The
paper underscores the importance of cell-level validation rather than relying solely
on population-level angular modulation indices. No direct action required for our
current analysis plan, but worth noting for any future CEBRA or population-level
directional analysis.

### 3. Evidence for predictive computations in a brain hierarchy during a visual search task

Authors not fully specified. 2026.
"Evidence for predictive computations in a brain hierarchy during a visual search task."
bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.09.717389v1

Posted: April 9, 2026 (within scan window).

**Findings:** Human fMRI study identifying hierarchically organised predictive
computations across visual cortex, parietal cortex, and frontal cortex during a visual
search task. Posterior areas encoded current sensory input; anterior areas encoded
predictions and prediction errors. RSP/posterior cingulate activity correlated with
prediction error magnitude.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Human fMRI, so species and experimental paradigm differ
substantially from our freely moving mouse preparation. The finding that posterior
cingulate/RSC activity correlates with visual prediction error is marginally relevant
to our paradigm: our lights-off epochs constitute a sudden violation of the expected
visual environment, which could generate a prediction error response in RSP. However,
the paper addresses visual search, not spatial navigation or HD coding. Included for
completeness as the only neuroscience paper from the April 7–10 window found in
searches.

## Summary of implications for hm2p

**New additions since the April 6 scan:** Two missed papers surfaced. The topographic
CA1–subiculum paper (#1) is the more important: it establishes that the HD and spatial
content of subicular projections to RSP depends on CA1 topographic organisation, which
constrains our interpretation of RSP spatial inputs. Specifically, boundary vector
coding in RSP may be more disrupted than HD coding if subicular topography is
disorganised — consistent with RSP having multiple input-specific spatial coding modes.

The hexadirectional analysis critique (#2) is a methodological note: if we apply
population-level directional analyses beyond basic tuning curves, we should validate
that any angular modulation is not an artifact of variance structure in the data.

**Penk+ gap confirmation:** Four scans (April 2, 4, 5, 6, and now 10) have returned
no published work on Penk-expressing neurons in RSP or any other cortical region in a
spatial navigation, HD, or visual cue processing context. Our study remains the first
to characterise this population.

**Trend:** RSP literature is active (averaging 2–4 new relevant papers per week over
the past two weeks), but the specific gap around cell-type-specific HD tuning in
genetically defined RSP populations is remarkably stable. No preprint has come close
to our study's core question across the scan period. The nearest competition remains
Jedrasiak-Cape et al. 2024 (cell-type-specific AHV in RSG, April 4 scan), which
addresses a related computation (angular velocity, not HD per se) in unnamed excitatory
subtypes.

**Total count this scan:** 1 moderately relevant paper, 2 tangential papers.
Cumulative across all April 2026 scans: 21 relevant preprints (6 highly relevant,
9 moderately relevant, 6 tangential).
