## Notable publication event (peer-reviewed paper, not a new preprint)

A search for recent RSC literature surfaced a Nature Communications article that
appears to be the published version of the Wei et al. (2025) bioRxiv preprint previously
noted in the April 2 scan:

**Wei, Couto, Kloosterman, Bonin. 2026.**
**"Anterior and posterior retrosplenial cortex form distinct visuospatial circuits
in the mouse." Nature Communications.**
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70762-z

This preprint (originally 10.1101/2025.06.24.661247v1) was covered in the April 2 scan.
The published version confirms peer-reviewed acceptance of the finding that anterior RSC
neurons display sharper position tuning and preferential sensitivity to fast visual motion,
while posterior RSC neurons show broader tuning and respond to slow, high-spatial-frequency
patterns. The companion bioRxiv preprint URL could not be verified as identical to the
published version (direct fetch blocked), so the Nature Communications DOI should be used
for citation purposes going forward.

**Updated citation for Stage 1 and analysis methods:**
Wei et al. 2026. "Anterior and posterior retrosplenial cortex form distinct visuospatial
circuits in the mouse." Nature Communications. doi:10.1038/s41467-026-70762-z

This replaces the bioRxiv preprint reference in any manuscript draft or code docstrings.

## Summary of implications for hm2p

**Another quiet window.** No new preprints relevant to the hm2p project were found in
the April 25–27 period.

**One actionable update:** The Wei et al. RSC A-P visuospatial circuits paper has been
published in Nature Communications and should be cited with the journal DOI rather than
the bioRxiv URL in any manuscript or documentation going forward.

**Cumulative picture after ten consecutive scans (April 2–27):**

- No study across ten scan windows (25 days) has characterised HD tuning properties of
  genetically-defined RSP neuron populations. The literature gap remains open and
  confirmed stable.
- No new paper on RSP HD cells + light deprivation has appeared since the Tian et al. MEC
  paper (April 2 scan). The methodological niche of miniature 2P with light deprivation
  in RSP (as opposed to MEC) remains specific to our study.
- The three most actionable papers from this scan cycle remain: (1) Laurent/Jacob
  multidirectional cells in RSC (April 2 scan); (2) active locomotion gating HD attractor
  dynamics (April 6 scan); (3) causal single-cell RSC connectivity (April 15 scan).

1. Separate moving vs stationary epochs in all light/dark HD tuning comparisons.
2. Document imaging position along the RSC anterior-posterior axis.
3. Consider orthogonal subspace decomposition (CEBRA) to test whether light/dark context
   separates from HD coding subspace.
4. Update citation for Wei et al. from bioRxiv to Nature Communications 2026.
