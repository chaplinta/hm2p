## Moderately relevant papers

### 1. Global structure of relational knowledge networks in retrosplenial complex; node-distance in hippocampus

Feld GB, Fernandez Velasco P, Gahnstrom CJ, Bernard M, Mian Q, Garvert MM, Dick F,
Gerchen MF, Spiers HJ. 2026.
"Global structure of new relational knowledge networks is represented in retrosplenial
complex, and node-distance in hippocampus." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.24.740522v1

**Findings:** Human fMRI study in which participants learned graph-structured relational
knowledge networks. Retrosplenial complex (RSC) encoded the global topological structure
of the network — overall graph geometry, not individual node identities. Hippocampus
encoded node-to-node distance within the graph. The two regions thus encode
complementary hierarchical levels of relational structure.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Extends the role of RSC beyond HD and landmark coding to
representing the abstract topological structure of environments. In a rose maze, the
global structure (radial arms, central hub) is precisely such a topological graph.
The prediction is that RSC population activity encodes the maze graph geometry, not
just instantaneous head direction. This is relevant for CEBRA population embedding
analyses: if the RSC population manifold mirrors the maze topology (as seen in the
coordinated representational drift paper from May 5), we should check whether Penk+ and
Penk⁻CamKII+ populations differ in how faithfully they represent graph structure vs.
angular position. Human fMRI limits direct translation, but the hierarchical split
(RSC = global topology, HPC = local distance) constrains what we should expect in
our mouse RSC data. This is from the Spiers lab; cite alongside their other RSC
spatial coding work.

## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 2. Deep conservation of head direction circuits in bees, ants and flies

Sayre ME, Gillet V, Ceberg N, Badalamente G, Pinzon-Rodriguez A, Griggs N,
Serratosa Capdevila L, Roberts RV, Sundberg Gunnarsson E, Szadaj F, Ellendula S,
Honkanen A, Narendra A, Heinze S. 2026.
"Deep conservation of head direction circuits in bees, ants and flies." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.29.741437v1

**Findings:** Comparative connectomics and anatomy of the central complex HD circuit
across three insect clades — bees, ants, and flies. The eight-column ring attractor
architecture, cell types, cell numbers, and columnar projection patterns are conserved
across all three species. HD is encoded as a sinusoidal activity bump around the ring
in all three clades.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Not directly relevant to RSP calcium imaging in mice. Provides
evolutionary context for HD circuit architecture: ring attractor HD encoding arose deep
in insect phylogeny and was conserved over hundreds of millions of years. This is
relevant background for the introduction when contextualising HD computation as a
fundamental neural circuit motif. The insect central complex is not homologous to
mammalian RSP, but the conservation of ring attractor dynamics supports the generality
of HD circuit principles being studied in our mouse RSP data.

### 3. Generative replay across hippocampal-neocortical circuits

Shearer CM, McDonald-Hill R, Guillaumin MCC, McGinnis OJ, Lopes Dos Santos V,
Perestenko PV, Dupret D, Barron HC. 2026.
"Generative replay across hippocampal-neocortical circuits." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.24.740539v1

**Findings:** Multi-unit electrophysiology and calcium imaging in freely moving mice
performing an inference task. During hippocampal sharp-wave ripple (SWR) events, V1
generates spiking sequences that encode relationships never directly experienced by the
animal. The probability and content of these V1 sequences are predicted by the
information content of immediately preceding hippocampal activity, consistent with
hippocampus providing a teaching signal that reorganises neocortical representations
via generative replay.

**Relevance to hm2p:** The hippocampal-neocortical teaching signal mechanism is
relevant background for the HPC-RSC axis: RSC receives strong HPC input, and an
analogous generative replay mechanism could reorganise RSC spatial representations
during rest. This is consistent with the Gonzalez/Buzsaki subspace communication
paper (April 2026 scan) and the finding that CA1-RSC subspaces are stable rather
than replayed. Methods (calcium imaging in freely moving mice combined with
electrophysiology) are relevant for future experimental design if extracellular
recording is added alongside 2P imaging. Not directly actionable for the current
dataset.
