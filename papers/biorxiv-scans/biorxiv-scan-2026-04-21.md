## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 1. Locomotion-invariant prefrontal–thalamic goal states organize spatially aligned episode-specific hippocampal maps

Golipour M, Yen S, Üstüner A, Ito HT. 2026.
"Locomotion-invariant prefrontal–thalamic goal states organize spatially aligned
episode-specific hippocampal maps." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.09.716651v1

Posted April 9, 2026. **Note:** This paper falls within the April 6–15 window that
should have been covered by the April 15 scan; it is included here only if it was not
captured previously.

**Findings:** When rats navigated the same maze under different goal configurations,
CA1 encoded goal state along a population dimension orthogonal to the spatial coding
subspace, keeping episode-specific maps spatially aligned rather than remapping. The
goal-state signal originated from a prefrontal–thalamic pathway (mPFC → nucleus reuniens
→ CA1). Silencing nucleus reuniens selectively abolished CA1 goal-state coding and
disrupted goal-biased pre-navigation spike sequences while sparing spatial coding.

**Relevance to hm2p:** This paper addresses hippocampal CA1 and mPFC, not RSP directly.
However, the orthogonal subspace framework is methodologically relevant: if our Penk+
and Penk⁻CamKII+ RSP populations also separate behavioural context (light vs dark) along
a dimension orthogonal to HD coding, this paper provides a conceptual precedent and
analysis framework (goal-axis vs spatial-axis decomposition via PCA/CEBRA). The nucleus
reuniens result is also a reminder that RSP is not the only thalamo-cortical node
involved in spatial context coding; however, it confirms the principle that prefrontal-
thalamic circuits gate how hippocampal spatial maps adapt to context — consistent with
the role of RSP as an integrative hub upstream of HPC.
