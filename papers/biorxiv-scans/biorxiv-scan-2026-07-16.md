## Tangentially relevant / methods papers

### 1. NMDA receptor-dependent Hebbian plasticity refines hippocampal spatial representations during two-dimensional navigation learning

Reshef R, Shahi M, Ho V, Ollivier M, Arac A, Cohen A, Yamin D, Tran et al. 2026.
"NMDA receptor-dependent Hebbian plasticity refines hippocampal spatial representations
during two-dimensional navigation learning." bioRxiv.
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.14.738487v1

**Findings:** NMDA receptor blockade during 2D maze navigation prevented the progressive
sharpening of CA1 place fields observed across learning sessions in control animals.
Hebbian NMDA-dependent plasticity underlies the gradual improvement in spatial
representation quality (peak firing rate, spatial information, field size reduction)
as animals become more familiar with the environment.

**Relevance to hm2p:** Peripheral: this is hippocampal place cell sharpening, not RSP
HD tuning. However, it is relevant background when discussing whether the HD tuning we
observe in RSP reflects a plasticity-dependent refinement of visual landmark anchoring
over the course of the session or across the experiment. If analogous Hebbian mechanisms
operate in RSP, early trials within a session may show weaker HD tuning than later ones —
a testable effect in our data by splitting trials within each 1-min light-on block.
