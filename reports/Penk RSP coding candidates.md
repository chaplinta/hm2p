# Compact Penk+ neurons point to sparse event detection

Penk+ L2/3 neurons in RSP look less like integrators of continuous navigational variables and more like **sparse detectors of discrete, coincident input**. The measured properties support this reading. Their membrane time constant is about a third shorter than in Penk⁻CamKII+ cells, sag (Ih) is larger, rest is more hyperpolarised and the dendritic tree is smaller. A cell built like this needs a few synchronous inputs to fire, ignores slow asynchronous drive, and once driven can fire faster (20 vs 13.5 max spikes). In vivo, Penk+ cells fire less, their firing clusters in time, and they are no more tuned than their neighbours to any continuous variable tested so far (HD, AHV, speed, position, light). That pattern fits a cell class whose information sits in rare, time-locked events that marginal tuning curves and linear GLMs cannot see. Two other readings remain open. One is slow tagging of context or episodes, which fits the longer calcium events. The other is that most of the measured in vivo difference is indicator, expression and rate artefact. That second reading has to be ruled out before anything else. For the 1 min light / 1 min dark maze, the main prediction is that any Penk+ light effect will be **transient and locked to light transitions, not a sustained light-vs-dark difference in tuning**. The same prediction applies to junction entries, turn onsets and stop/start transitions. All of this can be tested with event-aligned, session-pooled analyses that work at ~0.1 spikes/s and ~20 cells per session. A direct between-genotype comparison is weak with 11 vs 4 animals: an exact two-sided Mann-Whitney test at the animal level cannot reach p < 0.0015 even with complete separation, and real power is far lower. Each population therefore has to be tested against its own shuffle null first.

## Halved capacitance shortens the integration window by roughly a third

The ex vivo numbers (project data as supplied: 23 Penk+ vs 14 Penk⁻ cells, RSPd L2/3, groups recorded in different mice) can be combined into derived quantities that say more than the raw values. Membrane time constant is τ = R_in × C_in. Penk+ cells have **~26% higher input resistance (150 vs 119 MΩ) but ~half the input capacitance**, so their τ is about **0.63× that of Penk⁻ cells** (150 × 0.5 / 119). A shorter τ narrows the window over which EPSPs sum. The cell then responds to synchronous input more than to the same input spread out in time. The same numbers also imply a leakier membrane per unit area: capacitance scales with membrane area, so halving the area while R_in rises only 26% means specific membrane resistance falls to about 0.63×. More conductances are open at rest, and the larger sag (6.3 vs 4.2 mV) identifies HCN/Ih as one of them.

That points in the same direction. In CA1, Ih shortens EPSP time courses and normalises temporal summation ([Magee 1999](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn0699_508)). Modelling shows it selectively blocks summation of unsynchronised inputs while sparing synchronised ones ([J Comput Neurosci](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:JCNS.0000004837.81595.b0)). At hyperpolarised potentials it produces theta-band resonance ([Hu, Vervaeke & Storm 2002](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1113/jphysiol.2002.029249)) and contributes to spike-time precision in cortical neurons ([PMC3171884](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3171884/)). In mouse, unlike human, HCN1 is not ubiquitous in L2/3 pyramids ([Kalmbach et al. 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/312298v1.full)), so a mouse L2/3 cell with prominent sag is unusual. The −75 mV rest is mildly surprising, because Ih normally depolarises rest. Some additional resting K⁺ conductance probably holds these cells down. That has not been measured, but it fits the leakier membrane.

The rheobase difference turns out to come entirely from cell size. The steady-state voltage needed to reach threshold is rheobase × R_in: **70 pA × 150 MΩ ≈ 10.5 mV for Penk+ and 90 pA × 119 MΩ ≈ 10.7 mV for Penk⁻**. That is the same gap. This is a steady-state approximation that ignores sag during the step, but the conclusion holds. Penk+ cells are "more excitable" per picoampere injected only because they are smaller. Their voltage distance to threshold is no different, and with a rest 3 mV lower, threshold itself is probably about 3 mV more negative. Synaptic drive in vivo is set by how many inputs a cell receives as well as by R_in. A smaller, shallower dendritic tree carries fewer synapses. So the expected in vivo regime is **high gain per input but less total input**: a quiet cell that fires when a few inputs arrive together. Across brain areas, the smaller, higher-R_in, afferent-dominated principal class tends to be sparser and less often classically tuned than its larger, more integrative neighbour. Examples are piriform semilunar vs pyramidal cells ([eLife 73668](https://elifesciences.org/articles/73668)) and superficial vs deep CA1 ([Mizuseki et al. 2011](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.2894)). In awake L2/3 barrel cortex, sparse activity is explained mostly by narrow, diverse selectivity, not low excitability ([Ranjbar-Slamloo & Arabzadeh 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31243764/)).

Narrower spikes (1.8 vs 2.45 ms half-width) and higher maximum spike counts fit fast K⁺ repolarisation that lets Na⁺ channels recover between spikes. That gives a larger output range once the cell is driven, and better timing precision ([Kaczmarek & Zhang 2017](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/physrev.00002.2017)). The absolute half-widths are long for cortical pyramids, which probably reflects recording temperature or measurement method. Only the ratio (0.73) should be interpreted. Adaptation was not measured, which leaves open whether Penk+ cells sustain or only transiently follow strong input. That one measurement would most cleanly separate "event detector" from "rate tracker". All of the above treats Penk+ cells as a moderately more excitable, smaller, faster-integrating L2/3 pyramid. It does not assume they are the RSG low-rheobase class: that class has R_in ~400 MΩ and ~0.55 ms spikes ([Brennan et al. bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/673954v1.full)), well outside the Penk+ values.

## Rare, clustered firing matches the biophysics; long, small events do not

The in vivo profile (11 vs 4 animals, 450 somas, jGCaMP7f at ~9.6 Hz, CASCADE spikes; project data as supplied) agrees with the slice-based prediction in three ways. Penk+ cells fire less (**0.13 vs 0.19 spikes/s**) and are active in fewer 1 s bins (**8% vs 14%**). Their rate distributions are more skewed, meaning activity arrives in rare clusters. And they are **more heterogeneous across cells within animals**, which is what one expects when each cell is driven by a small, cell-specific set of inputs rather than a shared broad drive. Theory treats this kind of intrinsic diversity as useful rather than as noise. Diverse populations of mitral cells carry about twice the information of homogeneous ones ([Padmanabhan & Urban 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.2630)), with intermediate diversity optimal ([Tripathy et al. 2013](https://pnas.org/content/110/20/8248)).

The calcium events do not fit the picture. A fast-integrating coincidence detector should produce brief responses. Instead Penk+ events are **longer (3.3 vs 2.6 s), decay more slowly and are smaller (amplitude 4.6 vs 7.8)**. jGCaMP7f has a single-spike half-decay of ~0.27 s in vivo and ~0.52 s for 10 APs ([Dana et al. 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333836480_High-performance_calcium_sensors_for_imaging_activity_in_neuronal_populations_and_microcompartments)). Events lasting several seconds therefore need spikes spread over seconds, slower calcium clearance, or both. Bursts and plateau-driven complex spikes produce supralinearly *larger* fluorescence events ([Huang et al. 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/51675.pdf); [Siegle et al. 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/69068)). Smaller-and-longer events are therefore the opposite of a burst or plateau signature. They argue against the CA1-style BTSP reading, where plateaus produce large prolonged events ([Bittner et al. 2017](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aan3846)). The "bursty" label from the rate distributions most likely means rare events cluster in time, not that spikes are packed densely within an event. Two readings are left. Either Penk+ cells fire at low rates for the length of a behavioural episode (seconds), or the long, small events are mainly an indicator and expression difference.

The second reading is a serious concern because the populations were labelled differently: Cre-ON ADD3 in Penk-Cre mice vs the Cre-OFF construct 344. Expression level, nuclear filling and buffering all change event amplitude and decay. Cell biology adds two effects that pull in opposite directions. Narrower spikes let in less Ca²⁺ per spike, while a smaller soma concentrates whatever enters. The net effect on ΔF/F per spike cannot be predicted. CASCADE maps fluorescence shape to spikes using ground truth from other cell types ([Rupprecht et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-021-00895-5)). Spike-inference choices are known to change population-level conclusions ([Wei et al. 2020](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1008198)). Imaging also misses isolated spikes more than bursts ([Siegle et al. 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/69068)). So the 0.13 vs 0.19 rate gap is uncertain in size and possibly in sign. The lower Fano factor (0.40 vs 0.48) should not be read as more reliable firing either. At very low counts per bin, Fano factor is pulled down by the count floor and is not interpretable without rate matching.

A second labelling caveat changes how to read the heterogeneity. **Penk-IRES2-Cre labels L2/3 and L6 IT pyramids and also several VIP interneuron types** ([Hage et al. 2022](https://elifesciences.org/articles/71103); [Allen transgenic characterisation](https://connectivity.brain-map.org/transgenic)). Unless ADD3 uses an excitatory-specific promoter, the imaged "Penk+" population could include VIP cells. That alone would make it more heterogeneous. VIP cells are usually locomotion-driven, and the weaker Penk+ AHV and light modulation argue against heavy contamination, but this needs to be checked directly. Leakage can go the other way too: Penk+ cells with too little Cre to block the Cre-OFF construct would end up in the Penk⁻ group.

## The null results rule out continuous coding, not coding

The in vivo nulls are informative because of which variables they cover. HD tuning is weak and similar (~10% HD cells in both groups). Population HD decoding is at chance at matched N in both. Encoding GLMs with HD, AHV, speed, position and light explain almost nothing. Syllable information and junction-choice decoding are null in both. Every one of these analyses asks whether activity tracks a continuous or slowly varying variable *on average across time*. A cell that fires a few seconds-long events per minute, locked to particular moments, produces flat marginal tuning curves and near-zero GLM variance explained, even if every event carries information. Imaging-based selectivity estimates from sparse cells are also biased by event count ([Siegle et al. 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/69068)). Shuffle-corrected, rate-matched comparisons are therefore the minimum standard.

The RSC literature has plenty of candidate variables that the GLM leaves out. Freely moving rat RSC ensembles conjunctively encode route progress, position in the wider environment and left/right turning ([Alexander & Nitz 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.4058)). About **21% of rat RSC neurons are egocentric boundary vector cells** ([Alexander et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aaz2322)). RSC cells also respond to the egocentric position of geometric vertices ([Nat Commun 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51391-w)). Superficial RSC carries a sparse, orthogonal, place-like sequence code ([Mao et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-00180-9)). Head-fixed imaging puts many border, head-turning and speed cells in the superficial layer, and landmark cells are rarer in L5 than in L2/3 ([Fischer et al. 2020](https://elifesciences.org/articles/51458)). In a maze, the discrete moments a sparse coincidence detector could flag are junction entries, dead-end arrivals, turn onsets and locomotion start/stop. In this experiment they also include light-on and light-off. None of these is in the current model.

The AHV result needs separate handling. Penk+ cells show weaker AHV modulation depth, partly because of their lower rate. RSC carries AHV in complete darkness from vestibular input, and vision increases its gain ([Keshavarzi et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34788632/)). A fast-integrating cell could signal turn *onsets* transiently. That would be smeared at 9.6 Hz with jGCaMP7f and would appear as weak AHV tuning. Weak modulation depth therefore does not rule out turn-onset coding, but it gives no positive support for an AHV role either.

## Light cycles test transient against sustained coupling

The project's earlier results set the context. Raw "MVL higher in dark" effects did not survive occupancy and kinematic matching. Neither did Skaggs HD information, place information or a map-engagement measure. RSP spatial representations look preserved across light and dark, and the change shows up in exploration behaviour. A preserved idiothetic representation fits the classic finding that RSC inactivation selectively impairs navigation in darkness ([Cooper & Mizumori, via Mizumori lab](https://www.mizumorilab.com/publications)). Against that background, the Penk+ result is that light coupling is weaker across five measures, none significant on its own. All five measures compare sustained epoch-level activity or tuning between light and dark.

A sparse coincidence detector predicts something different. Light-on and light-off are the most synchronous change in sensory input in the experiment. They are also events with a defined time, which Ih and a short τ favour. Light-off adds a specific mechanistic angle. Withdrawal of visual drive can be followed by rebound depolarisation in Ih-rich cells, so offset responses are plausible (speculative for RSC). The prediction is a **peri-transition response in Penk+ cells (onset, offset or both) without a sustained epoch difference**. That would explain why epoch-averaged light coupling appears weak. If Penk⁻ cells show the reverse pattern (sustained epoch modulation, little transient), that is a population-level dissociation that does not need cell-matched tuning. If neither population shows transition-locked activity above shuffle, the event-detector reading loses its most direct test.

Enkephalin adds a slower layer that cannot be measured directly. In frontal cortex, DORs are present on 90–95% of ACC PV interneurons and their activation reduces feedforward inhibition ([Birdsong et al. 2019, eLife](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9242007/)). DORs suppress GABA release from both PV and SST boutons, through different signalling cascades ([Alexander & Bender 2025](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(25)00064-6)). In hippocampus, enkephalin co-released from glutamatergic pathways gates LTP by suppressing inhibition ([Bramham & Sarvey 1996](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/16/24/8123)). VIP-derived enkephalin drives DOR-mediated iLTD at PV synapses during social novelty ([Leroy et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-021-01124-y)). Neuropeptide release from dense-core vesicles needs high-frequency firing or bursts ([van den Pol 2012](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(12)00847-1)). Penk+ cells can produce those rates in slice. The small in vivo events, however, give no sign that they do so often. None of these opioid mechanisms has been shown in RSC. The notes found **no study linking Penk, enkephalin or opioid receptors to RSC function, spatial navigation or HD coding**. If release happens, it would act as a seconds-scale disinhibitory signal locked to events, which suits flagging salient moments for plasticity. It does not suit a moment-by-moment spatial code. The only part testable in these data is the behavioural side: whether Penk+ events concentrate at novel or first-encounter moments.

## Status of evidence and citations

**Established** (measured or in primary literature): the ex vivo and in vivo differences listed above (with sample and design caveats); Ih effects on summation and resonance; jGCaMP7f kinetics; burst supralinearity in calcium imaging; Penk-Cre labelling of IT pyramids and VIP cells; DOR suppression of PV/SST GABA release in PFC; enkephalin gating of hippocampal LTP and CA2 social memory; RSC coding of route, turn, boundary, landmark and AHV variables. **Inferred** (derived here): the 0.63× time constant, the equal threshold gap, the coincidence-detector regime, the contradiction between long/small events and bursting, and the reading of the nulls as tests of continuous variables only. **Speculative**: transition-locked light responses, rebound offset responses, episode tagging, enkephalin-mediated plasticity gating in RSC, and any link between Penk+ cells and novelty or engram allocation.

**Ex vivo design caveats:** the two groups were recorded in different mice, recording conditions are unknown, and the τ and threshold-gap derivations assume capacitance and R_in were measured comparably in both groups.

**Note on sources:** all four research notes were built from search abstracts and snippets; no full text was retrieved. The notes flag these citations:

- **DOIs taken from prior knowledge, not re-checked:** Magee 1999, Kalmbach 2018, Yiu 2014, Olshausen & Field 2004, Barth & Poulet 2012, Keshavarzi 2022, Alexander et al. 2023 (a Neuron review, not Nature Reviews Neuroscience), Cowansage 2014, Ziv 2013, Driscoll 2017, Rule 2019 and Miller 2019. Content of Mao 2020, Vedder 2017, Milstein 2021 and Grienberger & Magee 2022 was not re-verified.
- **Claims that could not be tied to a primary paper:** the claim that DORs increase SST→PV transmission came from an aggregated search summary. ## Conclusion

Taken together, the measurements describe a cell that **samples its inputs narrowly in time and in number**. That reframes the project's nulls: they show Penk+ cells do not encode continuous navigational variables on average, and leave open whether they mark discrete moments. The useful next step is to change the observable, not to add predictors to the GLM: from tuning to event alignment, and from per-cell tests to session-pooled tests against circular-shift nulls. Each population is first tested against its own null. The genotype comparison then becomes a comparison of effect sizes across sessions, reported with confidence intervals, because 11 vs 4 animals cannot support a stand-alone between-group test.

The indicator confound has to come first. If isolated-event decay differs between constructs, event duration and amplitude drop out as evidence, and only analyses based on event timing remain valid. The ex vivo adaptation measurement, which would take a few slice recordings, is the cheapest way to separate the event-detector and sustained-episode readings at the cellular level.

## Ranked candidate coding roles and the analyses that would detect them

Power arithmetic for all candidates: at an inter-event interval of ~31 s, one Penk+ cell gives **~2 events/min**, so 20 cells give **~40 events/min per session**. With one light transition per minute, a ±3 s window around transitions catches ~4 chance events per transition at population level. Over 20 transitions that is ~80 expected events, so a doubling to ~160 is detectable within a single session. Per cell, the expectation is ~8 events, so only large effects reach significance cell by cell. All tests are non-parametric. Within-session tests compare the observed statistic with a circular-shift null that preserves event count and inter-event-interval structure. Across-session tests use Wilcoxon signed-rank on per-session indices (and one session per animal as a sensitivity check). Between-group tests use Mann-Whitney at the animal level, reported with effect sizes.

**Indicator/expression controls that apply to every candidate:**

1. Compare isolated-event decay τ, baseline F and the fraction of nuclear-filled ROIs between groups.
2. Restrict to soma-classified ROIs.
3. Re-run each analysis on event *onset times only*, which removes amplitude and duration from the analysis.
4. Rate-match by thinning Penk⁻ events to the Penk+ rate before comparing any selectivity or information measure.
5. Use per-animal expression level (baseline F) as a Spearman covariate.
6. Check ROI morphology for small non-pyramidal (VIP-like) cells in Penk+ fields, and confirm the ADD3 promoter.

| Rank | Candidate role | Evidence strength | Key discriminating test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (baseline) | No computational difference; in vivo differences are indicator, expression and rate artefacts | Moderate, not excluded | Isolated-event decay and rate-matched re-analysis |
| 1 | Sparse detector of discrete, synchronous transitions (light on/off, junction entry, turn and locomotion onsets) | Moderate (inferred from biophysics plus the null pattern), untested | Peri-event time histograms pooled per session vs circular-shift null |
| 2 | Episode or context tagging at seconds-to-minutes scale | Weak to moderate | Epoch-identity decoding and within-session ensemble drift |
| 3 | Information carried in co-activation (assemblies), not single-cell tuning | Weak (coupling difference not significant) | Rate-matched pairwise co-activation and assembly counts vs shuffle |
| 4 | Novelty or plasticity gating, possibly via enkephalin | Speculative | Event rate at first visits and in early vs late session |
| 5 | Idiothetic HD/AHV relay, robust to darkness | Weak, largely contradicted | Turn-onset-aligned transients in dark epochs |

**0. The null hypothesis that every candidate must beat: indicator, expression and rate.** *Rationale:* different viral constructs, possible VIP inclusion, and count-dependent statistics (Fano factor, modulation depth, selectivity) can produce every in vivo difference except the rate itself. The rate is also uncertain, because CASCADE was calibrated on other cell types. *Supporting:* event amplitude and decay differ in the direction expected from expression; AHV modulation is partly rate-driven; none of the coding differences is significant. *Contradicting:* the ex vivo differences are real cellular differences (with caveats) and predict sparser in vivo firing on their own. *Prediction:* if this is right, isolated single-event decay τ differs between groups, and the rate, Fano and modulation differences disappear after thinning Penk⁻ events to the Penk+ rate. *Analysis:* control steps 1, 4 and 5 above, run before anything else.

**1. Sparse detector of discrete, synchronous transitions.** *Rationale:* a τ about 0.63× shorter, larger Ih and fewer inputs make the cell fire on coincident input and ignore slow asynchronous drive. In the experiment, the most synchronous input changes are light transitions and abrupt behavioural changes. *Supporting:* low rate, temporal clustering, cell-to-cell heterogeneity, failure of every continuous-variable model, and weak sustained light coupling. *Contradicting:* events are long (3.3 s), whereas brief transients are expected; no event-aligned analysis has been run yet. *Strength:* moderate inference, no direct test. *Prediction:* Penk+ population event rate rises within ±3 s of light-on and/or light-off and of junction entry, dead-end arrival, turn onset and locomotion start. Light-transition responses are transient: a transient-to-sustained index (peri-transition rate minus mean epoch rate) is higher in Penk+ than in Penk⁻ cells. *Analysis:* build peri-event time histograms from event onsets pooled across all cells in a session, using maze-graph events from `hm2p.maze.topology`. Compare with a circular-shift null and, for light, with pseudo-transitions at random phases. Compute a per-session z-score and run Wilcoxon across sessions within each group. Fraction of cells individually significant is a secondary measure. For junction events, match approach speed so that locomotion onsets cannot produce the effect. Use dark-epoch-only junction PETHs to separate visual drive from self-motion drive.

**2. Episode or context tagging over seconds to minutes.** *Rationale:* long, slow-decaying events, if they are real, mean low-rate firing sustained over behavioural episodes such as an arm traversal or a dwell. Sparse, less active cells are also the ones expected to drift: in hippocampus, less excitable place cells drift more ([Climer et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09245-y)), and excitability fluctuations drive ensemble drift ([Delamare et al. 2024](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/88053)). *Supporting:* event duration, heterogeneity, and a non-significant lean towards higher population coupling. *Contradicting:* duration is the measurement most exposed to the indicator confound. Penk+ cells are also more excitable ex vivo, while being less active in vivo, so it is unclear which form of "excitability" the drift result applies to. *Strength:* weak to moderate. *Prediction:* Penk+ population vectors decode light-epoch index and time-in-session tertile better than they decode position or HD. Ensemble similarity decays faster with time lag in Penk+ sessions. Event durations line up with behavioural episode boundaries (arm entry to exit) more than chance. *Analysis:* cross-validated epoch-identity decoding against a label-shuffle null ([Mau et al. 2018](https://www.bu.edu/hasselmo/MauSullivanKinskyHasselmoHowardEichenbaum2018.pdf) is the template). Compute ensemble-similarity vs lag curves ([Rubin et al. 2015](https://elifesciences.org/articles/12247)) with a bleaching control (detrended baseline F as a covariate) and with equal cell counts subsampled per session. Test episode-boundary alignment of event onsets and offsets with a circular shift.

**3. Information carried in co-activation rather than in single-cell tuning.** *Rationale:* coincidence detectors that share a sparse input stream should fire together more than chance even when none of them is tuned to a measured variable. Intrinsic diversity raises population information ([Padmanabhan & Urban 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.2630)). *Supporting:* slightly higher population coupling and noise correlations (not significant), and heterogeneity. *Contradicting:* population HD decoding is at chance, and the coupling difference may be a rate effect. *Strength:* weak. *Prediction:* after rate matching, more Penk+ cell pairs are co-active within 1 s windows than shuffle predicts. Detected assemblies are reactivated at specific maze locations or transitions (linking to candidate 1). *Analysis:* pairwise co-activation counts against a jittered-onset null (±5 s jitter preserves slow rate changes). Use PCA/ICA assembly detection with Marchenko-Pastur significance, and report per-session counts rather than fitted models (about 20 cells). Run rastermap ([Stringer et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-024-01783-4)) per session to look for traversal-locked sequences. Thin events before every comparison.

**4. Novelty or plasticity gating, possibly via enkephalin.** *Rationale:* Penk+ cells can reach the high rates that dense-core-vesicle release needs ([van den Pol 2012](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(12)00847-1)). Enkephalin suppresses PV/SST inhibition ([Alexander & Bender 2025](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(25)00064-6)) and gates plasticity at times of novelty ([Leroy et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-021-01124-y)). A low-threshold class could also be preferentially allocated to new ensembles, as relatively excitable neurons are in amygdala ([Yiu et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25102562/)), although that work concerns transient excitability within one cell type. *Supporting:* skewed, rare activity of the kind expected from a gating signal. *Contradicting:* small events argue against frequent bursting; there are no RSC opioid data; there is only about one session per animal; and peptide release cannot be measured. *Strength:* speculative. *Prediction:* Penk+ event rate is higher in the first minutes of the session, in the first dark epoch and at first entries into each maze cell, relative to later matched visits. Spatial preference after an event differs from before it. *Analysis:* compare event rate at first vs later visits per maze cell, with speed and dwell matched. Compare early vs late session with a bleaching correction. Test for a pre/post-event shift in occupancy-normalised maze-cell preference against a circular shift. Infer nothing about enkephalin itself from these data.

**5. Idiothetic HD/AHV relay that holds up in darkness.** *Rationale:* a fast-integrating, narrow-spiking cell can follow rapidly changing thalamic or vestibular input, and weak light coupling fits a non-visual drive. RSC AHV persists in the dark ([Keshavarzi et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34788632/)). *Supporting:* light coupling is lower (not significant). *Contradicting:* HD tuning is no stronger than in Penk⁻ cells; AHV modulation is weaker; population HD decoding is at chance; and the whole arc of the project shows preserved representations in both groups, not a Penk+-specific one. *Strength:* weak, largely contradicted. *Prediction:* if any relay role exists, it shows up as turn-onset-locked transients that are as large in dark epochs as in light, not as AHV tuning curves. *Analysis:* align events to head-turn onsets (|AHV| threshold crossings after at least 0.5 s below threshold), separately for light and dark epochs. Use a speed-matched circular-shift null and a Wilcoxon light-vs-dark comparison per session. Run it with event onsets only, so that GCaMP smearing and decay differences do not affect the result.
